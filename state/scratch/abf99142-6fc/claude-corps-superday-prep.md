# Claude Corps Super Day — Prep Sheet

Two 20-min interviews, scored independently. Last 5 min of each = your questions.
No memorized answers; the work is (1) picking your stories now, (2) practicing narrating thinking out loud.

---

## Interview 1 — "Keep Thinking" (live scenario)

They give ONE ambiguous workplace scenario, then 3 questions + follow-ups. What they're scoring:

1. **Stated ask ≠ underlying need** — do you dig under the surface request?
2. **Stakeholder handling** — skeptical / worried / affected colleague.
3. **Plain, honest narration** — saying "I don't know yet, here's how I'd find out" is a *strong* answer.

### Thinking routine to practice (use in the moment)

1. **Reflect back** — restate what I heard the ask to be (1-2 sentences).
2. **Ask what I'm missing** — who else is affected? what deadline? what already failed?
3. **Separate ask from need** — "They asked for X, but it sounds like what they need is Y."
4. **Sketch options with tradeoffs** — say them out loud, pick one, *say why*.
5. **Name the human** — the skeptical colleague gets a specific, respectful step, not "I'd communicate openly."
6. **Leave room** — "here's what would change my mind," or change my mind and *say so out loud*.

### Practice prompts (do 3-4 out loud, 5 min each, ideally with someone to push back)

- Your manager asks you to automate the weekly report for the team. A senior teammate says it's a threat to their job and it's wrong priorities.
- A stakeholder asks for "a dashboard." Half the org has no reliable data. You're the only analyst on the team.
- Leadership wants the volunteer intake process moved online before the grant deadline. Volunteers are older, skeptical, and the current process is messy.

**Score yourself after each:** Did I separate ask from need? Did the skeptical person get a real, specific treatment? Did I say what I was unsure about and how I'd find out?

## Interview 2 — "Keep Making Change" (mission & culture)

Four question areas. Each needs ONE specific, real story. Specificity is the point; unflattering is fine.

| # | What they're probing | Your story (fill in) |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Why this work, and what it *cost* you (time/money/relationship/easier path) — plus someone who thought it was the wrong call | _ |
| 2 | Stuck with something hard/messy/disappointing when no one was checking — or what you took away if you left | _ |
| 3 | Something you kept doing after it stopped being new, with no external accountability | _ |
| 4 | Why *this path* over the others you seriously considered — what tipped it | _ |

### Story shape (keep it honest, not performance)

- **The event** — 2-3 sentences of what actually happened, with a concrete detail (name-free) that proves it's real.
- **The messy middle** — where it got harder than expected. This is where weight lives. Skip the tidy setup.
- **The cost or consequence** — what it actually took.
- **Where you're at** — one honest sentence, no moral bow.

**Anti-pattern to avoid:** a rehearsed pitch. If your story sounds like something you'd say in every interview, it's the wrong story.

### Also prep (not scripted, just considered)

- What excites you about this role, and what *worries* you about it. (The worry is more interesting.)
- The last 5 minutes of EACH interview: have 2-3 questions ready, e.g.
  - "What does a rough vs. great first 90 days look like at a host org?"
  - "How do hosts actually rank fellow preferences — what moves it?"
  - "What's something the program has changed in past cohorts based on evaluation?"

- [ ] 2-3 own questions for each interview
- [ ] Nothing to read or review on the day. Deliberate pauses are fine and read well.
