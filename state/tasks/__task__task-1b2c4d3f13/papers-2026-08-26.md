## 1. Quantum computing

**ON-FOCUS — Optimal Quantum Algorithm for Ground-State Energy Estimation with a Guiding State (with matching lower bound)**
Stacey Jeffery, Freek Witteveen — [arXiv:2608.24494](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.24494) · companion lower bound: Rolando D. Somma, Ronald de Wolf — [arXiv:2608.24493](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.24493)
Posted as a simultaneous matching pair: an algorithm that cuts the query count for guided ground-state energy estimation by a factor of $log(1/γ)$ over the previous best, meeting an existing lower bound, and a paper proving that bound is tight — closing a named open question (Mande & de Wolf). Why it matters here: ground-state energy estimation *is* the cost question for any quantum chemistry program (Phase 3, OpenEvolve — DFT/QM scoring tiers), and a factor of $log(1/γ)$ is exactly the regime gap between "oracle overlap is decent" and "oracle overlap is rare," i.e., where trial-state quality is poor. Transducer-based composition (no error-reduction overhead) is also the right primitive for layering estimation inside larger circuits. A clean "the problem in this niche is now settled" result — the standard to measure future claims in the same space against.

**ON-FOCUS — Nearly Optimal Amplitude Estimation at any Depth**
Jona Erle, Bálint Koczor — [arXiv:2608.24434](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.24434)
Amplitude estimation is the workhorse behind quantum speedups in estimation-heavy workloads (Monte Carlo, overlap certification, trial-state verification). The new thing: a depth-tunable family ($M$ circuit depth × $N$ repetitions) that reaches the nearly optimal $M^2N ∈ \tilde O(ε^{-2})$ tradeoff **uniformly over the full angle range, including the boundaries** $λ→0, π/2$ where prior depth-tunable results degrade to classical sampling — and it needs no ancilla and no controlled Grover ops, which is significant for real hardware. It spans classical sampling ($M=1$) to the Heisenberg limit ($M=Θ(ε^{-1})$) as one continuous knob. Read it next to Jeffery–Witteveen (§1): both are "the depth/depth-tunable regime gets a rigorous statement" papers, and together they say the early fault-tolerant toolkit's estimation layer is now well-understood — cost estimates in this space were previously hand-wave, and those estimates just got audit-grade numbers.

**ON-FOCUS — Scalable Lindblad Noise Learning via Stochastic Tensor-Network Simulation**
Alejandro R. Ramos Ramos, Maximilian Fröhlich, Aaron Sander, Robert Wille, Martin Eigel, Michael Hintermüeller, Patrick Gelß, Sebastian Pokutta — [arXiv:2608.24668](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.24668)
Lindblad (dissipative) rate learning is the missing piece between "I have a quantum device" and "I know how its noise behaves at scale" — existing methods are stuck at small system sizes because each optimization step means re-solving the Lindblad equation. The new thing: a Tensor Jump Method (stochastic tensor-network) + gradient-free optimization combination that scales to 160 sites (global model) or 16 sites (site-resolved), *plus* three exact theorems (variance characterization, monotone purity decay, and a counterintuitive result that cost-function std decreases with system size, so fewer trajectories are needed as you scale). The theory side is the stronger result — the "fewer trajectories needed for large systems" guarantee is the kind of thing that converts a computational trick into a usable measurement protocol. Directly relevant as the noise-characterization layer that sits underneath any error-correction or mitigation workflow for the hardware Phase-3 QM tiers will lean on.

**ADJACENT (1 of 1 used) — Provable Quantum–Classical Separation for Continuous Gibbs Sampling**
Enrico Olivucci, Mariia Sobchuk, Sehmimul Hoque, Jeffrey Hnybida, Kyungho W. Kim, Ala Shayeghi, Pooya Ronagh — [arXiv:2608.24527](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.24527)
One of the rarer things in quantum ML right now: a *provable* separation claim for a core sampling task rather than an empirical "quantum kernel beat RBF" benchmark. Ronagh, Shayeghi, Kim et al. (Quantinuum-adjacent group) argue for a quantum advantage in continuous Gibbs sampling with a formal reduction, and they classify it under cs.LG alongside the usual suspects. Whether the separation holds up to scrutiny and how strong the constants are matters more than the headline, but the *type* of result is worth tracking — the field still needs more "provably where the quantum advantage is" papers and less "we benchmarked a small kernel" papers. Note that the 08-25 quantum-kernel result in this same window (Peng et al., [2608.24631](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.24631)) explicitly states its own result "establishes predictive and representational value rather than computational quantum speedup" — the field is self-correcting about what it *can* claim, and that honesty is the signal.

## 2. Machine learning

**ON-FOCUS — Effective Learning Rate Governs Loss Dynamics in Language Model Pretraining**
Zihan Liu, Ruiheng Zheng, Shaobo Zhang, Changxin Tian, Kunlong Chen, Zhiqiang Zhang, Lei Wu — [arXiv:2608.24814](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.24814)
The central object in pretraining dynamics is two dials — learning rate and parameter norm — and the claim here is that they collapse onto a single coordinate: the **effective learning rate** (LR / ‖θ‖). When ELR is matched across runs, loss trajectories overlap tightly across optimizers, architectures, datasets, and scales, with mean collapse errors below seed-to-seed variance. This is not a new architecture or trick — it is a *reparameterization of the problem* that subsumes LR scheduling, weight decay, and Hyperball under one variable, and it makes a functional scaling law transferable *across* norm-control methods (which is normally the gap every scaling-law paper hits). For anyone running a local 27B training or fine-tuning pass on a tight hardware budget, ELR is the right invariant to hold fixed when sweeping hyperparameters — it tells you how much of your tuning was actually just re-scheduling. It also explains "delayed acceleration," a recurring but previously ad-hoc effect of norm control, as a consequence of the ELR schedule it induces rather than a separate phenomenon.

**ON-FOCUS — Reading Is Not Using: Retrieval, Judgment, and the Design of AI Financial Research Workflows**
Miao Liu, Zhizhe Liu — [arXiv:2608.24842](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.24842)
Holds the focal-firm information fixed and varies only the *unrelated* context from 2k to 128k tokens, then measures whether a risk disclosure still influences the model's investment judgment. Answer: the disclosure's influence drops to the noise floor even when direct retrieval of it remains accurate — the model *retrieves* the information but does not *use* it in judgment. It reproduces across model families and, critically, in experiments removing real disclosures from actual 10-K filings, and stronger models only *postpone* the gap rather than close it. The most actionable finding is the one about architecture: chunk-and-summarize pipelines evict relevant information, while a targeted structured restatement placed adjacent to the decision point restores it. This is a direct, empirically grounded warning against a naive "stuff the context, summarize, decide" workflow for any agent doing judgment under long context — and it is exactly the kind of failure mode that is invisible to retrieval-precision metrics. Read it next to the Granqvist et al. judge result (08-24): that paper says *how to build the evaluation*; this one says *why the evaluation is catching a real failure*, not a benchmark artifact.

## 3. Recursive self-improvement

**ON-FOCUS — Metaⁿ: Recursive Self-Improvement through Emergent Depth**
Zae Myung Kim, Young-Jun Lee, Seungyeon Jwa, Dongyeop Kang — [arXiv:2608.24735](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.24735)
The framing problem this solves is real: self-improving agents edit their own process, which is unstable — the system must leave some layer untouched to stay stable, which caps realized meta-depth at ~2. Metaⁿ's answer is elegant in its mechanics: keep the **meta-operation Ω fixed** and recurse on its *input* instead. Each layer of Ω reads the full trace and code of the solver stack below it, writes a strategic pre-process plus a library of callable helpers, and moves to the next layer. Because Ω never changes it cannot destabilize the system, and because its input strictly grows, each successive layer reasons from a strictly higher vantage than the one below. Depth is determined by a convergence test, not preset. The ablation finding worth taking: *most of the gain from recursion comes from the conditioning each layer passes to the next, not the depth itself* — and distinct role specializations emerge with depth even though no prompt prescribes them. It scores above zero on ARC-AGI-2 where prior self-improving agents do not, outperforming them on eight benchmark families across two backbones. Open-source (Minneapolis NLP group). Read it next to Phantom Gains (2026-08-21): Phantom Gains says "audited self-improvement gains are often measurement artifacts"; Metaⁿ is the strongest positive claim in this record this month — the two together define the current honest range.

**ON-FOCUS — RecurSE: Bounded Recursive Self-Evaluation for LLM Rubric Judges**
Kaiyuan Liu, Ziyuan Zhuang, Rongxiang Weng, Jieping Ye — [arXiv:2608.24231](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.24231)
This is the missing third piece in the judge/measurement triad with Phantom Gains (audit the null) and Granqvist et al. (build the aligned judge). It removes external gold supervision entirely from the RL reward: the judge's own output generates the signal for its optimization, in a *bounded* recursive loop. Two non-obvious findings: (1) **interface decoupling** — the checker's scalar reward must be structurally isolated from the judge's verdict tokens, because without that decoupling the model learns a degenerative token-copying shortcut that inflates self-assigned rewards. That is a concrete architectural failure mode anyone building a self-improving judge will hit. (2) **boundedness is not a bug, it is the design constraint** — unanchored recursive learning is essentially bounded, and a monitor (their Pairwise Advantage Validity) to identify the optimal early-stopping window is the correct interface, not an aspiration for unbounded recursion. Across Qwen3.5-9B, Gemma-4-E4B, and Qwen3.6-27B it generalizes to held-out domains and the co-evolved judge outperforms frozen checkers, external meta-judges, self-consistency, and scaled teacher distillation. The practical takeaway: bounded RSI is the right regime for judges, and the monitor, not the model, is the key component.

**ON-FOCUS — CAFE: Self-Improving Search Agents Need Co-Evolving Feedback**
Boyang Liu, Senjie Jin, Peixin Wang, et al. (18 authors) — [arXiv:2608.24794](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.24794)
The central ablation is the result: improving only the agent or only the critic eventually plateaus, whereas alternating updates between the two (Coupled Agent–Feedback Evolution) continues to improve. That is a direct, empirical answer to the question Yang et al. (08-24) raised theoretically — *which component of the loop must be big/good* — with the answer being "both, and they must co-evolve." The mechanism is non-trivial: they initialize feedback-conditioned recovery from the base agent's *own* failure trajectories, then couple online RL (comparative feedback estimate via a prompt-level call-skip success gap) with offline preference optimization from matched success/failure rollouts. The gains hold across all six out-of-domain benchmarks and reduce answer-level hallucination. This is the strongest direct evidence yet in the RSI record that the agent/critic split is not a *capacity* question (small vs. large) but a *coupling* question — which reframes what "small fleet, strong judgment" (Faraday pattern) actually requires: the judge must be updated alongside the policy, not frozen.

**ON-FOCUS — Recursive Experiential-Working Memory Evolution for Long-Horizon Agent Harnesses ("Recuris")** *[catch-up — posted 08-25 evening, inside the 2608.248xx range but near the boundary of the 08-25 window]*
Zhaochen Yu, Yingcheng Wu, Zhenfei Yin, Kaiyuan Chen, Zhe Zhao, Mengdi Wang, Shuicheng Yan, Ling Yang — [arXiv:2608.24876](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.24876)
The problem: in long-horizon tasks, growing task history obscures current state and misaligns skill invocation — the agent remembers what it did, not what is currently true. Recuris splits memory into Working Memory (tracks task progress, guides skill selection) and Experiential Memory (accumulated skill library), and couples them so execution produces *structured evidence that localizes failures to specific memory components*. A fixed Meta-Agent then uses that evidence to make validation-gated updates to the skill library. The numbers are large: +17.8 pts on GPT-5.6, +15.6 on Claude Opus 5 (to 87.9% on tau-bench), +32.2 pts on the longest tasks, and common long-horizon failures down by up to ~80%, across four benchmarks and ten models. Open-source (Gen-Verse group). The "localize failures to memory components" detail is what makes this more than a memory-architecture paper for the purposes of this record — it is the evidence that *structured memory decomposition* is the precondition for recursive improvement to work at all, which is a direct input to how the OpenWorker harness should track state across its own runs.

**ON-FOCUS — Prime Agent: A Self-Improving RLM Harness** *[catch-up — posted 2026-08-24 evening, inside the 08-24 run's snapshot top but apparently missed]*
Seth Karten, Alex L. Zhang, Kevin Thomas, et al. (11 authors) — [arXiv:2608.23552](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.23552)
A harness rather than a model: a persistent IPython REPL (Recursive Language Model abstraction), continuation of history/memory/skills/prompts across trajectories, recursive subagents with agent-to-agent coordination, and a human-facing management view. What it claims is an ARC-AGI-3 RHAE Best@1 jump from 30% to 95.5% from harness improvements alone, plus matching or exceeding native harnesses on long-context coding, GPU-kernel generation, and autonomous nanoGPT speedruns. The 30→95.5 jump is almost certainly a harness-measurement artifact and should be read that way: it is the strongest single demonstration in this record of the Phantom Gains warning (harness improvements are not model self-improvement; the score is measuring the harness, not the model underneath). Open-source (Prime Intellect). Worth keeping in the running record as the counter-example to any "self-improving agent" claim that does not specify *which part* improved — model, harness, or both.

### Cross-topic observation
This run's strongest RSI items — RecurSE, CAFE, and Recuris — all converge on the same empirical finding from different angles: **the judge/checker component and the memory component are the load-bearing parts of the loop, not the model itself.** CAFE says so directly (one-sided improvement plateaus; co-evolution continues). RecurSE says it structurally (the checker must be decoupled and its boundary is the design constraint, not a bug). Recuris says it architecturally (localizing failures to memory components is what makes recursive updates safe). For the OpenWorker fleet running on a local 27B model, the practical implication is concrete: the investment in this loop is in the *harness's memory structure and the judge's training loop*, both of which are within reach — the model weights are the fixed substrate, and that is consistent with the Faraday pattern (flagged 2026-08-23) and the Yang et al. asymmetry finding (08-24). On the quantum side, Jeffery–Witteveen/Erle–Koczor together say the estimation primitives' cost envelope is now rigorous, which means cost estimates in the OpenEvolve Phase 3 tiers (PySCF, CHGNet) can be quoted against a known theoretical floor rather than an empirical hand-wave — the audit discipline Davoudi–Stryker (08-24) demanded is now, partially, available.
