## 1. Quantum computing

### Fault-Tolerant Quantum Computation with Adversarial Errors
**Nikolas P. Breuckmann, Louis Golowich, Umesh Vazirani** — https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.16857

My take: this is the one paper in today's quantum batch I'd actually call a result, not an engineering advance. Prior FTQ theorems essentially assumed noise that is either local-and-stochastic or only corrupts a vanishingly small fraction of qubits per step. Here the adversary may flip an almost-linear number of physical qudits (`N^{1-o(1)}`) *every* timestep, i.e. a global, non-Markovian, worst-case noise model — and the depth overhead is only `\bar{N}^{o(1)}`, with poly-size overhead. The mechanism is a new subsystem product code family with local testability, plus recursive composition to drive the logical dimension to a constant. Two reasons this matters: (a) it directly neutralizes the "correlated noise kills logical qubits" objection that has held back the circuit-to-Hamiltonian route to quantum PCPs, and (b) Vazirani co-authoring a fault-tolerance result is a genre change, not incremental theory.

### Hundred-hertz quantum circuit iteration rate in a reusable neutral-atom array
**Liang Chen, Wen-Yi Zhu, Dong-Qi Ma, et al.** — https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.16815

My take: neutral-atom machines have been winning on scale and coherence but losing on *throughput* — the "reconfigure the array" step is the bottleneck. This is a 10-qubit demonstration with non-destructive readout (99.7% retention) plus atom-reuse, hitting 101 Hz raw qCIR. Not a scaling breakthrough, but the framing is the point: they benchmark throughput the way classical chips benchmark MHz, and report a normalized Fisher-information-rate of 57.7 Hz as a hardware-level KPI. That's a useful primitive for whoever is deciding between neutral atoms and superconducting architectures for VQE/quantum-ML workloads specifically.

### Designing Quantum Error Correcting Codes to fit decoders via Reinforcement Learning
**Omer S. Sella, Robert Pinsler, Thomas Heinis** — https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.15754

My take: the standard workflow is "here's a code family, please find a good decoder." This inverts it — fix the decoder architecture, and have PPO *generate* Bivariate Bicycle codes that make that specific decoder shine under depolarizing noise. Conceptually important because decoder quality and code structure are co-determined (same Pauli-frame information, different inductive biases), and this is a rare case where the *code* is treated as the learnable object rather than the decoder. It's a first cut on a small family (BB codes) and a single noise channel, so don't over-estimate — but the direction is worth tracking.

### Resolving and resetting the charge environment of single T-centers in silicon p-i-n waveguides
**Chaoshen Zhang, Hanbin Song, Lukasz Komza, et al.** — https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.15993

My take: T-centers are one of the most promising telecom-band spin-photon sources on a *manufacturable* photonics platform, but their linewidth is dominated by charge noise. This demonstrates a physical reset — above-band illumination collapses the local charge ensemble — and reports the narrowest unheralded linewidth to date on an integrated T-center (128 MHz on the best of 46 emitters, 3.5× median improvement). Also reports coherent optical Rabi with 20 ns coherence. This is the kind of "boring hardware" result that actually unblocks scalable quantum networking rather than one more spin-squeezed photon source.

### (Notable lower-tiebreak)
- **zenDot: An LLM-integrated quantum TCAD platform for semiconductor quantum-device design and optimization automation** — [2608.16048](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.16048). LLM-in-the-loop quantum device design is an emerging niche; worth a look if you're on the hardware-design side.
- **Classical Verification of Quantum Advantage via Clifford Obfuscation** — [2608.15963](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.15963). Interesting if you care about the epistemics of QEC/QA claims; the abridged abstract doesn't let me vouch for the substance, so I'm not ranking it higher.

## 2. Machine learning

### Proteus: Incremental Memory Activation for Long-Context Sequence Modeling
**Reza Bayat, Ali Behrouz, Vahab Mirrokni, Aaron Courville** — https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.16844

My take: this is a genuinely clean architectural observation. The whole line of memory-based long-context models (SWLA, Titans, Hope-Attention, Comba) shares an assumption: the memory state is *statically sized* across the sequence. The authors argue early tokens have no compression pressure and thus take up memory that later context needs; their fix is to *grow the effective capacity of memory as the context grows*, so early tokens are under an early bottleneck that forces compression, and late tokens see the newly-opened capacity. It's a zero-parameter mechanism that drops into existing memory backbones and reports wins that grow with context length. This is a design-principle paper in the best sense — "schedule capacity, not just tokens" — one I expect to appear in the next batch of long-context baselines.

### Le Critique: Privileged Value Functions for LLM Reinforcement Learning
**Siddarth Venkatraman, Matthieu Dinot, Laurence Aitchison** — https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.16739

My take: GRPO's "sample a group of rollouts, take the mean" trick is now effectively the default for RL on LLMs, and the field has quietly decided critic-based RL is not worth the infrastructure. This is a direct challenge to that: (1) they design a *privileged* value function so the critic sees task-relevant signal that the policy doesn't — without injecting bias into the policy gradient — and (2) TETHER adaptively interpolates between group-relative and value-function baselines depending on how accurate the critic is, which is a clean answer to the classic "critic is worse than rollout means during training" failure mode. The claim "competitive with or better than mean-baseline GRPO" is what actually matters, because that's the bar every RL-on-LLM paper is implicitly held to.

### GEO-Flag: Detecting and Measuring GEO-Optimized Web Content
**Junjie Chu, Ye Leng, Mingjie Li, Yun Shen, Xinyue Shen, Yang Zhang** — https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.16824

My take: GEO (Generative-Engine Optimization) is the emerging "SEO for LLM answers" field, and this is a detector *and* measurement instrument for it — 21 pages, 21 tables. Matters because if GEO-optimized content is now a non-trivial fraction of the web, every downstream LLM retrieval and RAG evaluation is biased, and we need to be able to *see* that. The methodological contribution (detection, not just a benchmark) is the differentiator versus one more "how LLMs rank results" paper.

## 3. Recursive self-improvement

### BaT: Towards Self-Evolving Medical Research Agent with Stage Rubrics
**Junqi Liu, Yufan He, Yexiao He, Pengfei Guo, Dong Yang, et al.** — https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.16211

My take: this is the closest thing today to a *closed-loop* RSI paper with measurable numbers. BaT couples a Stage-Bank data pipeline that synthesizes curriculum outside the policy loop with BiCuRL (bilevel curriculum RL) — the outer level picks what to practice *next* via a fixed held-out evaluation, the inner level (policy update via GRPO) improves on that stage. The claim that a **9B agent beats Claude Opus 4.6 + Claude Code on AutoMedBench-Lite** (79.6 vs 77.5 overall) is the strongest empirical RSI number I've seen in this window, and it's on a domain where the agent's task is explicitly "medical-research" rather than toy puzzles. Also notable: they pre-register the ablation program and *report the failures at full prominence* rather than hiding them — for the field.

### JailbreakSkill: Scaling Automated Red-Teaming with Reusable and Ever-Evolving Skills
**Xiaoyu Wen, Jiajia Li, Zhida He, Peng Yu, Chenxu Wang, et al.** — https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.16465

My take: this is a concrete instance of *recursive self-improvement with a closed loop*: an attack library, a diagnosis step, an evolution step (refine, combine, discover new skills), and an "add back to library" step. Reported gains are large — a 48.6-point macro-ASR lift against GPT-5.4 on AdvBench, plus emergent behaviors like "reframing a direct request as an unfinished document-completion task" that the agent produced *itself*, not a human prompt. Even if you're on the defensive side, that's the shape of threat the defender needs to plan for: not a fixed jailbreak set, but a skill library that grows from past attempts. This is one of the more consequential RSI papers of the last few days in the practical "security matters" sense of consequential.

### Competing at Every Price Point with Agentic Evolution over a Menu of LLMs (RoboPhD)
**Andrew Borthwick** — https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.16207

My take: this inverts the framing. Instead of "train the next model," it treats a *menu of existing LLM endpoints* as the genome and a *meta-agent* (RoboPhD) as the evolutionary loop. Training pools are at most **100 examples**. It claims Pareto-domination of the frontier on DS-1000 (execution-checked code) and PaperFindingBench (scientific retrieval), including beating both the top-scoring and lowest-cost competing points. The significance is not the specific leaderboard positions (small tasks, single author) but the *mechanism*: agentic program evolution over a menu, driven by cost-constrained fitness, from small data. If the claim reproduces, the same skeleton applies anywhere "you can afford N LLM calls per answer" is the design constraint — which is most deployed agentic systems.

### Prior Audit-Repair Context Shifts LLM Verifier Thresholds Toward Leniency
**Parsa Mazaheri, Kasra Mazaheri** — https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.16003

My take: small paper, important result. They measure that when the *same* LLM is wired as both checker and fixer (or two LLMs in a checker→fixer pipeline), the checker gets *more lenient*: 15-of-15 "model × wording" combinations show 2.8–11.5 pp fewer false alarms, with a *completed* audit→repair episode in context lowering false alarms even further. Signal-detection analysis localizes the shift to a movement in the threshold, not a change in discrimination. This matters directly for recursive self-improvement pipelines: if your verifier gets more forgiving because the *pipeline shape* tells it to, that's a systematic failure mode of the loop — not just a per-model property.

- **VCE-Skill: Enhancing Skill Self-Evolution with Version-Change Experience** (Chen, Ye, Wang, Wang, Wang, Xu) — [2608.16544](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.16544). Skill self-evolution with version tracking; incremental but on the right track.

- **Mechanist: AI as a Scientific Instrument for Discovering the Mechanisms of Intelligence** (Wang, Fang, Qiao, Xu, Xu, et al.) — [2608.12036](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.12036). "Work in progress" but the positioning — AI used to *explain* intelligence — is the direction the field is heading.
- **Physics of Agents: Statistical Mechanics Predicts Collective Behavior of AI Agents** (El, Paeng, Dinc, Su, Erdogan, et al.) — [2608.16578](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.16578). If autonomous-research loops scale, this is the kind of theoretical scaffold you'll need.

### Cross-topic observation
The RSI and ML lists overlap deliberately: BaT, RoboPhD, and JailbreakSkill all lean on RL + evaluation + curriculum machinery (BiCuRL/GRPO, value functions, skill libraries). Today's batch suggests the field is coalescing around a small number of primitives — **curriculum selection, verifiable reward, and reusable skill/state artifacts** — that are being tested in different domains (medical, code, security, trading). If those three primitives are the substrate, the interesting open question for the next quarter is not *which* domain wins, but whether *verifiability of the reward* is the real bottleneck — and BaT's "pre-register and report failures at full prominence" is the strongest methodological signal I've seen on that today.
