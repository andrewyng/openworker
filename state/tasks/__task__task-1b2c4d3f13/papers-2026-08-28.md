## 1. Quantum computing

**ON-FOCUS — The Find Rows and Columns and Decode algorithm for quantum expander codes**
Dimiter Ostrev — [arXiv:2608.27211](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.27211) · submitted 2026-08-27
Fault-tolerant quantum computation's dominant overhead is as much the *decoder* as the code. This paper adapts the classical "Find Erasures and Decode" algorithm to quantum expander codes so it runs in **linear time and logarithmic parallel depth**, sidestepping the stabilizer-generator subset bookkeeping that the prior "Small Set Flip/Find" methods required — and, on the reported comparisons, it corrects more errors while demanding less expansion. New and material: it is a clean *algorithmic* cost result for the decode step rather than a new code, and it sits in exactly the family the 08-27 "Certified Decoding of QEC" paper opened (both are the decode-cost half of the OpenEvolve Phase 3 QM-budget question). The honest caveat is that "corrects more errors" is not a full threshold statement, so it reads as a strong engineering result rather than a proof of an improved error threshold — the kind of result you fold into a budget estimate and then test, not yet one you bet a logical qubit on.

## 2. Machine learning

**ON-FOCUS — Understanding Evolution Strategies for LLM Reasoning: Broader Reasoning Coverage than GRPO**
Yunpeng Ba, Zhi Zheng, Yue Xie, et al. — [arXiv:2608.27351](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.27351) · submitted 2026-08-27
Evolution Strategies (ES) are a memory-efficient post-training paradigm, but *why* they beat GRPO had never been explained. This paper does the mechanism work: ES leads to **broader reasoning coverage** (verifier-projected Jensen–Shannon diversity drives higher Pass@K), GRPO suffers **entropy collapse**, and — crucially — despite large whole-model parameter drift, ES's gains concentrate in a **sparse subset of larger-magnitude updates**, with held-out evaluation showing this does *not* produce catastrophic forgetting. New and material: it repositions ES as a *distinct* reasoning post-training paradigm, not a cheaper stand-in, and the finding is directly actionable for the OpenWorker fleet's constrained-hardware training: ES gives reasoning gains at a smaller memory budget, on a sparse update, without the forgetting that would otherwise make it unusable on a fixed 27B. A sequential GRPO-ES combo that inherits GRPO's Pass@1 strength plus ES's Pass@K is a ready-made off-the-shelf move.

**ON-FOCUS — Circuit Condensation: Post-Training that Concentrates a Behavior's Causal Circuit**
Sai Adith Senthil Kumar — [arXiv:2608.27254](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.27254) · submitted 2026-08-27
Mechanistic circuits found on a frozen model run to hundreds of edges, which makes them hard to inspect, verify, or compare. Circuit Condensation instead *post-trains* the model to concentrate a behavior into a smaller causal graph (prune low-attribution edges, fit a low-rank adapter to the remainder, keep the cut only if task and general capability survive): **8.1× smaller on average, up to 316×** across eight models. New and material is the diagnostic about *why* it shrinks: re-running the search without any weight updates produces *larger* circuits in 29 of 32 settings, so it is the weight update — not the search — that does the work. The indirect-object-identification test then isolates a *sufficient sub-circuit* (24 heads, 17 documented, vs. 61 for the frozen baseline) that tracks the original's next-token distribution and error pattern. On-FOCUS because it is the "produce an auditable, minimal, verifiable sub-mechanism rather than a sprawling reconstruction" impulse that the record's judge/trust thread (Granqvist, Recuris, FrontierChallenge) has been driving toward.

**ADJACENT (1 of 3 used) — LLMs Can Design Near-Optimal OR Algorithms**
Jackie Baek — [arXiv:2608.27296](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.27296) · submitted 2026-08-27
A frontier-LLM-as-algorithm-designer study over well-specified operations-research problems (inventory control, queueing networks, assortment optimization). With a single untuned prompt and a fixed-compute Python sandbox, the strongest model (gpt-5.6-sol) **matches or outperforms the best existing method on almost every instance** — and this holds even at "level 2," where the model returns a fixed algorithm before it sees any evaluation instance. New and material: the result is that frontier LLMs can already be a serious *empirical baseline* for algorithm design in well-specified OR, and that performance improves sharply across models released less than eight months apart — the capability is moving fast, which is exactly the signal to watch in the "models that improve their own capabilities / automated research" question. ADJACENT because FOCUS.md names no algorithm-design project driving it, but it bears directly on the RSI watch scope and the algorithmic-superintelligence lineage in the record.

## 3. Recursive self-improvement

**ON-FOCUS — AgentFold: Closed-Loop Agentic Search for Protein Folding Model Design**
Mingquan Liu, Jiangyu Chen, Hanqun Cao, et al. — [arXiv:2608.26747](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.26747) · submitted 2026-08-27
The decisive test of "can an LLM agent autonomously improve a large, tightly-coupled scientific ML system": AgentFold treats folding-model development as a **closed-loop search over executable code variants** on a 2,000+ line ESMFold-style codebase, proposing hypotheses, implementing and debugging code, evaluating variants, analyzing outcomes, and storing *both successful and failed interventions* in structured memory, with an MCTS-style policy allocating resources across promising branches. Over ~80 variants, ~5,000 GPU-hours, and ~170M LLM tokens it improves the best lDDT by **7.5% over independent Codex proposals** and beats a random-search control. New and material is what the intervention traces revealed about *which* self-improvement sticks: stable gains come from **early, soft, learnable priors and gated refinement**, while direct geometric perturbations and geometry-conditioned feedback often *destabilize* training. Directly on-FOCUS: it is the real-world analogue of OpenEvolve's own self-improving-agent loop for molecular model design, and it confirms two of the record's core priors at once — external structured memory (successes *and* failures) is the precondition for a working loop, and the architecture of the improvement step matters more than raw search breadth.

**ON-FOCUS — Verify Smarter, Evolve Further: Efficient Harness Evolution through Behavior-Aware Verification**
Jinghan Xu, Yikai Zhang, Aili Chen, et al. — [arXiv:2608.27311](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.27311) · submitted 2026-08-27
Agent harnesses (how the model gets its instructions, tools, and runtime) matter as much as the model, but evolving them is verification-expensive: score every candidate on a fixed task set and you waste rollouts on unrelated behaviors and let aggregate scores hide specific regressions. HarnessLens derives candidate harness edits from execution trajectories and **selectively verifies each candidate only on behavior-relevant tasks through an attributable-evidence gate**, exploring the task space and user-configurable harness components jointly. Across three harnesses and four benchmarks it raises held-out performance by **7.6–13.6%** while consuming far less evaluation budget than baselines. New and material: it is a concrete, budget-aware *harness-evolution* loop that improves the harness without touching model weights — a direct on-topic confirmation of the record's repeated "the structure around the model is the load-bearing lever" prior (KOPE 08-26, AgentFold today), now in the agent-harness domain rather than memory or folding.

### Cross-topic observation
Today's thread is narrower than 08-27's but sharper, and it reinforces one conclusion from every angle: **the structure around the model — memory, harness, decoder, post-training update — is where the gain is, and the "know when to stop / when it's real" layer is what keeps the gain honest.** Quantum (§1) hands back the decode-cost floor for the QM tier the OpenEvolve Phase-3 audit demanded. ML leans on the *update mechanism* rather than the architecture: ES reasoning (2608.27351) gains come from a sparse, forgetting-free update; Circuit Condensation (2608.27254) gains come from weight updates, not search — both say the same thing in different words. RSI is the strongest on-topic cluster: AgentFold and HarnessLens are two independent demonstrations that a well-structured *improvement loop* over fixed or expensived resources beats brute-force search, and AgentFold's "soft priors + gated refinement beat direct geometric perturbation" result is a real, testable prior for how OpenEvolve's own operators should be shaped. The one new ADJACENT item (LLMs designing OR algorithms) is the "the model is getting good at designing algorithms" signal — worth watching as the frontier of the "models improving research" question, but not yet a FOCUS-driver.
