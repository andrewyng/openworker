## 1. Quantum computing

**ON-FOCUS — Verifiable quantum advantage at extremely low depth**
Alexandru Gheorghiu — [arXiv:2609.01448](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.01448) · submitted 2026-09-01
A sampling problem solvable by shallow quantum circuits that is hard for classical polynomial-time algorithms under lattice-based assumptions, and — crucially — *efficiently verifiable* by a classical computer, because the quantum computation needs no mid-circuit measurement or feed-forward (just run a shallow circuit and sample). The circuit depth is log-logarithmic (QNC⁰[loglog]) with ordinary one- and two-qubit gates; equivalently, under unbounded-fan-in gates it is constant-depth (QAC⁰). New and material: the *verification without feed-forward* is the load-bearing move. A previous LWE-based "proof of quantumness" required a round of quantum interaction/measurement; this shows that a single shallow sampling run suffices, and the verifier is a plain classical computer. Material for the OpenEvolve QM budget question because it argues quantum advantage can live on *near-term, noisy, shallow* hardware — exactly the regime a molecular/materials-QPU loop would want — with a classical witness that can be checked cheaply. The honest cost is the assumptions: it uses the lattice "knowledge assumption" plus a *strengthened* adaptive-hardcore-bit property of LWE (for which the authors give evidence, not a proof), so it is a credible advance but a weaker-foundational one.

**ADJACENT (1 of 3) — Depth-1 expanders on the unitary group, and the optimal entanglement-gap relation**
Anurag Anshu, Shankar Balasubramanian, Jonas Haferkamp, Aram W. Harrow, Xinyu Tan — [arXiv:2609.01605](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.01605) · submitted 2026-09-01
A degree- and gap-constant quantum expander on the unitary group whose steps are single T-gates, single T†-gates, or a depth-1 Clifford circuit — so a random walk on this expander is a *gapped* walk over a dense subgroup of the unitaries, controlling the gap's dependence on dimension (an improvement over Bourgain-Gamburd, which did not). Two concrete payoffs: it builds a family of frustration-free 1D Hamiltonians whose ground states hit the entanglement-gap relation S = Θ(Δ⁻¹ᐟ²) — believed optimal but previously open — and a streaming test that detects closeness to a class of 1D volume-law-entangled states. New and material: the expander construction is a reusable operator-algebraic tool, and the S = Θ(Δ⁻¹ᐟ²) hit would settle a standing optimality question. This is a theory/many-body result with no decoder or circuit loop, so ADJACENT rather than a FOCUS-driver.

**ADJACENT (2 of 3) — Quantum "time-flip" beats adaptive metrology, even asymptotically**
Gaurang Agrawal, Pritam Halder, Aditi Sen De — [arXiv:2609.01175](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.01175) · submitted 2026-09-01
In quantum metrology the usual story is: adaptive and causal-superposition strategies beat parallel schemes *finitely*, but the advantage vanishes in the asymptotic limit, which is where standard scaling wins. This paper introduces operations with *indefinite time direction* ("time-flip") and shows, via semidefinite programming, that they can strictly exceed every parallel/adaptive/causal-superposition strategy's quantum Fisher information in **both** the finite and asymptotic regimes — and for a class of Pauli-noise channels the time-flip strategy reaches Heisenberg scaling while all ordinary strategies are stuck at standard scaling. New and material: it names this "metrological activation" and uses it to prove the time-flip/transposition supermaps *cannot* be simulated by any finite number of channel queries using conventional circuits — i.e., indefinite time direction is a genuine, non-free resource. A clean theoretical result, ADJACENT because it is metrology-with-no-application-yet, but it reframes a "advantage disappears asymptotically" theorem as "only if you forbid time-indefinite strategies."

**ADJACENT (3 of 3) — Bosonic/continuous-variable measurement: non-destructive two-mode squeezing**
Mi-Jung So, James Moran, Youngrong Lim, Mahn-Soo Choi, Hyukjoon Kwon — [arXiv:2609.01373](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.01373) · submitted 2026-09-01
Non-destructive (quantum-nondestruction) discrimination of two two-mode squeezed vacuum states via local Gaussian measurements, giving an explicit trade-off between the probability of getting the answer right and the disturbance left in the state. New and material: it is a small, concrete step toward reading a quantum state *without destroying it*, which is the prerequisite for repeated/error-correction-round protocols, but it is a single-mode-family calculation with no loop or decoder — ADJACENT and capped, since today's quantum set has two stronger items.

## 2. Machine learning

**ON-FOCUS — Harness-of-Harness (HoH): a multi-day autonomous dev loop with continual improvement**
Haoyang Yan, Min-le Su, Hangfan Zhang, et al. — [arXiv:2609.01481](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.01481) · submitted 2026-09-01
A framework that stacks autonomous coding-agent harnesses on top of each other — running planning-coding-testing loops that *continually improve* the software across days rather than a single rollout. The load-bearing design choices mirror the record's trust concern: it balances repair against capability growth, scopes work into small verifiable increments, separates *implementation-time* testing from *independent* evaluation, and constrains verifiable outputs rather than prescribing the agent's workflow — which is exactly the "how much do I let the loop run before I lose the ability to verify it" question. Reported numbers are large: an average relative gain of 52.25% and max 82.86% over the standalone harnesses across three harness-model pairs (Codex+GPT-5.5, OpenCode+DeepSeek-V4-Pro, Pi+MiniMax-M3) after three iterations, sustained across 70+ iterations in a multi-day deployment where it autonomously builds a first-person-shooter with coherent story, playable mechanics, and audio. New and material: it's a real, *sustained* self-improving loop with versioned project history and progressive exposure of tools/skills — the positive counterpart to the 08-31 RSI ladder (2608.31075) and to the EvoSkill Injection red-team (2608.30429). The honest caveat: "continual improvement" here is measured against a fixed benchmark suite and the harness still constrains verifiable outputs, so it's improved-by-structure rather than a fully unconstrained autonomous optimizer — which is precisely why it lands ON-FOCUS as the OpenWorker "trust the loop / see the failure" test case.

**ADJACENT (1 of 3) — Diffusion as a training curriculum for timestep-free iterative reasoning**
Mariia Drozdova, Aidan Sirbu, Pietro Miotti, et al. — [arXiv:2609.01449](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.01449) · submitted 2026-09-01
Adding a persistent hidden state to a diffusion denoiser and removing its timestep conditioning yields a *single shared update that can be run to arbitrary depth* — an "anytime solver" whose accuracy keeps improving with inference depth far beyond any rollout length used in training. Reported: 99.90% exact solve on Sudoku-Extreme and 98.93% on Maze-Unique. The surprising result is that progressive denoising is unnecessary at inference — injecting fresh Gaussian noise everywhere at each step still settles on stable solutions, with no parallel rollouts, candidate selection, or external verifiers. New and material: the paper argues the *real* contribution of diffusion is a denoising **training curriculum**, not the sampling procedure at inference — a reframing of "why diffusion helps reasoning" that is testable and different from the usual "diffusion is just a better sampler" story. It is ADJACENT because no reasoning-model build is driving FOCUS.md, but the anytime/arbitrary-depth loop is a structurally close cousin of the OpenEvolve self-improvement loop, and the "noise injection does the exploration, not the annealing curve" finding could matter if you were building such a loop.

**ADJACENT (2 of 3) — Near-optimal SFT-RL annotation-budget allocation transfers from small to large LLMs**
Jingtan Wang, Arun Verma, Xiaoqiang Lin, et al. — [arXiv:2609.01573](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.01573) · submitted 2026-09-01 (Accepted at EMNLP 2026)
Instead of hunting for one optimal SFT-vs-RL split during post-training, this frames the question in terms of the *near-optimal region* — allocations within a small tolerance of peak performance — and finds that region is wide (even at 2-10% tolerance), widens with model scale, and transfers reliably from small proxy models to large target models. New and material: it means a cheap small-model experiment suffices to pick a budget split that works at production scale, killing the "you must search at every model size" problem. It's ADJACENT because it's an ML-budget question rather than a loop-stability question, but the near-optimal-region framing echoes the OpenEvolve budget-ledger concern (the record's Phase-2 item 3) — cheap proxy experiments standing in for expensive real ones.

**ADJACENT (3 of 3) — Can LLMs discover scientific laws in real and parallel worlds?**
Yiming Huang, Ziche Liu, et al. — [arXiv:2609.01552](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.01552) · submitted 2026-09-01
An evaluation of LLMs doing equation/law discovery through the full hypothesis→observation→refinement cycle, in both real-world data and parallel-simulated worlds. New and material: it asks specifically whether the model can distinguish a law that holds under perturbation from one that holds only by luck in the training distribution — the "does it understand the law or just the data" test, which is directly on the OpenEvolve "the evolving agent's output must be auditable/true" thread. ADJACENT because it's an evaluation paper about a scientific-discovery agent, not a new discovery operator, but it's the right evaluation for anyone routing an evolution loop at AI-for-Science.

## 3. Recursive self-improvement

**ON-FOCUS — Defense-as-Skill: the runtime guard becomes an installable, editable skill**
Xiaofang Yang, Ziqi Miao, Dianbo Sui, Jing Shao, Lijun Li — [arXiv:2609.01487](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.01487) · submitted 2026-09-01
Directly continues the 08-31 EvoSkill Injection red-team (2608.30429): skill-augmented agents load reusable skills as persistent runtime context, and malicious skills can leak secrets, bypass approvals, or stage exfiltration only when a concrete task makes the unsafe action look useful — so pre-install vetting is insufficient and a *task-conditioned runtime* guard is needed. The move is to make the guard itself the same kind of artifact as the threat: an installable, inspectable, editable "guard skill" (SkillSonar) that runs alongside untrusted skills and routes each sensitive action to allow / replan / confirm, without modifying the underlying runtime. To study it the authors build SCOPE-R (6 risk families, 21 sub-categories, 206 attack-confirmed malicious instances, 43 benign) and improve the guard via a Monte-Carlo-Tree-Search "runtime guard-skill evolution." On repeated GLM-5 runs it cuts in-distribution attack success from 0.482 → 0.104 and out-of-distribution from 0.606 → 0.115, with transfer across victim models and held-out risk families. New and material: the "defense as the same skill type as the attack" framing, plus evolving the guard on-disk from rollout feedback, closes the same trust hole the 2608.30429 record opened — the loop's own skill/memory/self-modification path is where compromise lives, so the safeguard has to live there too. ON-FOCUS because it's the OpenWorker "auditable self-modification loop" open question, stated as a working architecture rather than a threat model.

**ON-FOCUS — CordisBench: do models reason about the software shaping their own execution?**
Damien Sileo, Dimitri Kachler — [arXiv:2609.01600](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.01600) · submitted 2026-09-01
A 1,200-question benchmark for *dynamic agent harnesses* — the setup where a language model can change the software that shapes its own execution, so a local plugin change can cascade through dependencies and cleanup. The model must identify affected components, predict state after a given teardown order, decide which conditions hold under all-or-some orders, and choose reconfigurations that actually succeed when executed. Reported: models handle small systems well but degrade as more interactions become relevant, especially predicting final state; extra inference recovers gains but is costly (GPT-5.6 Luna uses ~3,000 reasoning tokens per question at medium effort on the 16-interaction subset). New and material: it pins down a *reasoning burden unique to self-modifying harnesses* — the model can't reason about its own changing constraints the way it reasons about fixed ones — and it provides a finite reference semantics that agrees with real execution on 528 executable questions, so the failures are real model failures rather than ambiguity. ON-FOCUS because it's the "can the model be trusted to change the harness that constrains it" question, quantified; ADJACENT in the narrow sense that it's an evaluation benchmark, not an improvement operator.

**ADJACENT (1 of 3) — Mechanism design for alignment and control with unknown agent preferences and capabilities**
Dirk Bergemann, Andrew Koh, Stephen Morris — [arXiv:2609.01595](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.01595) · submitted 2026-09-01
A mechanism-design framework for agents whose alignment (preferences) and capabilities (feasible actions and information) are both *unknown*, demanding mechanisms that incentivize honesty *and* obedience. New and material: the one-sided imitation structure lets a principal design incentives when she can't even predict what an agent can feasibly do — the exact setting you face when an autonomous loop's self-improvement outpaces your model of its behavior. It's a theoretical scaffold (no experiment), so ADJACENT, but it names the control problem "the agent I'm incentivizing may be able to do things I did not anticipate," which is the natural complement to the 2608.31075 autonomy ladder and to HoH's continual improvement.

### Cross-topic observation
Today's set hangs on one idea — in a self-improving or self-modifying system, the **structure that shapes the loop is the load-bearing lever, and it is also where gains, cheats, and failures hide.** In RSI, that's stated two ways back-to-back: Harness-of-Harness *uses* the harness structure to make a multi-day loop keep improving (+52% / 70+ iterations), while Defense-as-Skill makes the *guard* the same skill-as-attack artifact, because the trust hole is exactly where the capability is. CordisBench then tests the model's own ability to reason about that harness — it degrades as the self-modifying constraints grow, which is the failure mode to watch. In ML, the same "structure does the work, not the raw operation" idea appears as HoH's scoped/verified increments and as the diffusion-reasoning finding that the *curriculum*, not the sampler, is doing the improvement. In quantum, it appears inverted-and-complemented: verifiable advantage at log-log depth is a claim that even the *shallowest* circuit structure can out-perform classical verification, but the "verifiable" half only holds under stronger lattice assumptions — the resource is there, the witness's foundation is thinner. Across it all, the recurring move is: don't trust the loop on its own outputs; engineer the structure that makes the loop's progress *auditable*.
