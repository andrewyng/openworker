## 1. Quantum computing

**ON-FOCUS — Resource-Efficient Bio-Molecular Docking on a NISQ-era Digital Quantum Computer**
Tianqi Chen, Adrian M. Mak, Jianguo Li, Jian Feng Kong, Chandra Verma, Sebastian Maurer-Stroh — [arXiv:2608.19868](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.19868)
The one item in the live batch that lands directly on an open FOCUS question — *what replaces the `surrogate-offline` docking proxy in OpenEvolve*. They recast docking as maximum vertex-weighted clique on a compatibility graph and encode it for a NISQ variational circuit via a full-basis Bloch-vector encoding, with a useful theorem (a global minimizer of the objective can always be a pure product state — which is what *licenses* the unitary variational circuit at all) and a real IBM-executed run rather than simulation-only. Read as a candidate design for the openEvolve scoring stack: not a better Vina, but a pattern for what a quantum-assisted docking adapter should look like when the adapter lands.

**ON-FOCUS — An Irreducible Quantum Advantage in Aligning World Models with Reality**
Josep Lumbreras, Hailan Ma, Jayne Thompson, Mile Gu — [arXiv:2608.19779](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.19779)
A hard separability result at the quantum/ML boundary: there exist *classical* true worlds (long memory, outcomes depending on far-past events) for which **every finite classical world model provably misaligns the optimal policy of the virtual world from the real one** — and each admits a single-qutrit quantum world model that reproduces it exactly. This is "quantum ML" in its strongest possible form, because the advantage is irreducible-by-memory-size, not just scaling: no bigger classical simulator closes the gap. If the construction generalizes beyond the single-qutrit toy, it is the kind of result that re-argues the case for quantum simulators inside agent/RL loops — directly relevant to the OpenEvolve stack's world-model ambitions.

*ADJACENT (1 of 3) — Counterexamples to the fractional coloring conjecture for triply efficient shadow tomography*
Jędrzej Stempin, Santiago Llorens, Felix Huber — [arXiv:2608.20113](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.20113)
Short paper, one detail worth a line: the counterexamples were "found with GPT Sol 5.6." That is a live, citable instance of an LLM generating nontrivial negative results in quantum information theory — mild evidence against the FormalTCS bottleneck prior (flagged 2026-08-21), and a data point for the Eureka-claim ledger (still unverified, 2026-08-20).

## 2. Machine learning

**ON-FOCUS — Inducing Task Models from Computer-Use Traces**
Yucheng Jiang, Zora Zhiruo Wang, Ruishi Chen, Diyi Yang — [arXiv:2608.20319](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.20319)
Directly on the OpenWorker question of what an agent stack should *learn from its own sessions*: TMI discovers latent tasks in unconstrained, multi-threaded computer-use traces and induces a paired model per task — a recursive goal decomposition plus a control-flow procedure model. The number that matters is the extrinsic one: skills derived from the induced task models improve held-out task accuracy by **30.0%** over the strongest workflow-induction baseline — the first clean measurement that *how you structure recovered experience* (task+control-flow vs. flat step summaries) is the variable, not just having the trace at all. That is the difference between a memory (MemTrapBench, flagged 2026-08-21, showed raw retrieval is net-negative) and a structured one.

**ADJACENT (2 of 3) — MidTool: Mid-training Data Synthesis for Agentic Tool Use**
Fengqing Jiang, Yite Wang, Boyi Liu, Zhaoyang Wang, Canwen Xu, Zhewei Yao, Radha Poovendran, Yuxiong He — [arXiv:2608.20314](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.20314)
Relevant to the local-27B fleet question: tool-use capability is treated as a mid-training-stage concern, synthesized from **real-world tool APIs and MCP skills**, and shows Qwen3-Base models improve on BFCL, tau2-Bench, and MCP Universe under both SFT and RL post-training. The takeaway for this stack is not the 4B/8B models — it is that the field's current answer to "small local model is bad at tool use" is *dedicated mid-training on tool-shaped data*, not a bigger inference-time model. Worth remembering as the design-space point when deciding how much tool-surface to budget.

**ADJACENT (3 of 3) — Pandora's AI Model Routing Box: Efficient Allocation with Costly Value Estimation**
Adam Fisch, Shubhendu Trivedi, Fantine Huot, William W. Cohen, Michael Kaisers, Mirella Lapata, Kate Larson, Jacob Eisenstein — [arXiv:2608.20316](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.20316)
Closes the Pandora's-Box gap in model routing: estimating each specialist's value *costs computation*, and they give closed-form value-of-information expressions deciding per-query whether the expensive estimator is worth calling — and a decentralized variant where specialists decide whether to self-assess before bidding. Useful prior for the OpenWorker tool-surface/cost question (flagged in FOCUS.md as "tool-surface budgeting on the local 27B"): the routing decision is itself an active computation-budget allocation problem with a known-good formal structure, and their central finding — expensive estimation far less often, matching quality — is the empirical shape an internal router should aim for.

## 3. Recursive self-improvement

**ON-FOCUS — Training AI Scientists to Replicate Research (Replica + Faraday)** *[catch-up — caught on 2026-08-23, first time in this record]*
Damon Falck, Samer Sabri, Anja Surina, Thom Foster, Anya Sims, Sam Devlin, Dylan Rogers, Tantum Collins, Kaloyan Aleksiev, Louis Kirsch, Edward Hughes (Inherent) — [arXiv:2608.13331](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.13331) · [inherentlabs.ai](https://inherentlabs.ai/research/training-to-replicate)
Posted 2026-08-13 — inside the 08-16 digest window, missed by it; surfaced in the news cycle today, hence flagged now rather than re-suppressed. A **27B-parameter** "AI Scientist" (Faraday) post-trained with long-horizon RL on Replica — 310 figure-replication tasks from 100 ML / AI-for-science papers — that **beats Claude Opus 4.8 and GPT-5.5** on held-out replication, while directing a *much larger* frontier coding agent (GPT-5.5/Codex) as its tool. Three things make this ON-FOCUS rather than a headline: (a) the 27B scale is the same class as the local-model fleet — evidence that scientific *judgment* layers can be small even when the execution tool is a frontier model; (b) **no hand-coded evolutionary harness and no test-time reward are needed** — which is the strongest possible endorsement of the OpenEvolve direction, since OpenEvolve's premise is that the evolutionary outer loop is the primitive; (c) the reward-side contribution — an auto-generated per-task rubric judge with multi-sample aggregation and turn-level credit assignment to stabilize non-verifiable-domain RL — is a concrete pattern for the OpenEvolve verification question ("what replaces the surrogate-offline docking proxy" is the same problem as "how do you get a reliable reward when there is no ground truth").
