## 1. Quantum computing

**ON-FOCUS — Fault-tolerant co-design: transversal gates + magic-state distillation on a spin-qubit shuttling bus**
Pau Escofet, Andrii Semenov, Niall Murphy, et al. — [arXiv:2609.02641](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.02641) · submitted 2026-09-02
Proposes a multi-qubit *spin-qubit shuttling bus* that does three jobs at once, where most FTD literature optimizes only one: robust syndrome extraction, *transversal* two-qubit gates between an **arbitrary** number of logical qubits (giving all-to-all logical connectivity via coherent spin shuttling), and an ancilla-sharing scheme that packs several logical qubits into one logical element to shrink footprint. The decisive piece is applying Quantum Reverse Mapping at the logical level to lay out a **15-to-1 magic-state distillation** circuit on this same hardware, and extending the bus from a 1D track to a 2D grid of tracks, which shortens inter-qubit distance and lowers logical error. New and material: it's not another threshold number or decoder tweak — it's a *principled co-design* bridging the physical, QEC, and logical-computation layers of the stack. Directly ON-FOCUS because it answers the "can one real, tunable QPU platform actually run an autonomous QML/materials loop" half of the OpenEvolve Phase-3 question, the half the 08-28/31 decode-cost and generalization papers left dangling.

**ADJACENT (1 of 3) — Amplitude estimation without power-of-two schedules: a parallel QAE at the adaptive limit**
Farrokh Labib — [arXiv:2609.02715](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.02715) · submitted 2026-09-02
Non-adaptive Quantum Amplitude Estimation fixes Grover depths in advance so every circuit runs in parallel, but it has cost more queries than the best adaptive methods. Replaces two conventional choices — subspace-based post-processing and power-of-two depth ladders — by the *exact* maximum-likelihood estimate (one matrix mult per batch) and a geometric ladder with ratio r≈1.45. The result: a fully parallel, deterministic estimator hitting total query complexity 2.8–3.1/ε at 95% confidence, matching chebAE's *average-case* complexity (the best adaptive benchmark) while needing only 0.21/ε max sequential depth against chebAE's 2.9/ε — i.e. all the parallelism without losing the adaptive bound. New and material: the "~1.45" is not tuned, it's *derived* — power-of-two (doubling) is exactly the fastest depth growth at which the data can still tell neighboring candidates apart, so the denser ladder is the edge of confusion. Genuinely on the algorithm-QML axis because amplitude estimation is the workhorse behind QML estimation, and a parallel-at-advantage estimator is exactly what a noisy-scale-QPU loop wants.

**ADJACENT (2 of 3) — Exponential-speedup polarization stabilization for long-haul quantum networks**
Jinyi Du, En Teng Lim, Xingjian Zhang, et al. — [arXiv:2609.02841](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.02841) · submitted 2026-09-02
Fibre quantum networks sending polarization-entangled photons need continuous basis stabilization, but the usual feedback paths trade off noise (bright classical reference light) against slowness (entangled-photon feedback, which also interrupts the entanglement channel to calibrate and scales as the *product* of link losses over distance). Combines wavelength-bracketed probing with switch-enabled path decomposition so spectrally adjacent entangled-photon sidebands track the central channel's polarization response *without interrupting it*, changing acquisition-time scaling from product-of-losses to sum-of-losses, and transfers the calibration settings in-band. New and material: demonstrated on a **133 km fibre testbed with closed-loop stabilization >24 h without any classical reference light** — a real, long-distance, self-calibrating quantum-network control loop. ADJACENT because it is networking hardware, not a QEC/algorithm lever for the OpenEvolve QM budget, but it is the "scalable long-distance entanglement distribution" half of the same platform question.

**ADJACENT (3 of 3) — Conditional validity of deployed quantum ML classifiers**
Roberto Fernández-Barrios, Iker Pastor-López, Asier González-Santocildes, Pablo García Bringas — [arXiv:2609.02781](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.02781) · submitted 2026-09-02
A deployed quantum-ML classifier can fail two ways simultaneously: target data shift, and the model *itself* being randomized by finite-shot quantum evaluation (the Gram matrix is a random draw). Develops an information-conditional, "fail-closed" auditing framework that returns *supported / refuted / unresolved* verdicts with anytime-valid per-claim error control, and on a Higgs-to-tau-tau collider benchmark shows that stable classifier metrics do *not* guarantee valid signal-strength inference — a fixed-template profile loses coverage even in shift-free controls when template statistical uncertainty isn't modeled. New and material: it propagates *every* realized finite-shot Gram matrix through refit/calibration/threshold selection across 30 frozen quantum-kernel deployments, and — crucially, in the right register for the OpenEvolve thread — *claims no quantum advantage* while exposing exactly where a QML audit must land. ADJACENT (it is an evaluation/audit, not a new QML operator), but it is the "auditable deployed QML output" test on the same thread 2608.31117 opened.

## 2. Machine learning

**ON-FOCUS — Repo-To-Skill (DisCo): distilling 1,000 ML repos into a 5,000+ skill library that carries an AI-research agent**
Jianlyu Chen, Yuyang Hu, Hongjin Qian, et al. — [arXiv:2609.02749](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.02749) · submitted 2026-09-02
Framing: autonomous ML-research agents now run end-to-end (model + harness of planning/execution/memory/verification), but the *operational knowledge* — the know-how that separates knowing a method from making it work — is "written for human readers and too large to load," so it gets rediscovered inside every run. Distills it into compact, **verified** skills, usable both *task-agnostically* (AREX-Skill Library: 5,000+ skills from 1,000 widely-used ML repos, 20 areas / 178 capability families) and *task-orientedly*. Reported: holding the GPT-5.5 backbone, research harness, and execution budget fixed, the skill-equipped research agent scores **134.3% higher on MLE-bench, 34.4% on PaperBench, 9.2% on FrontierCS, 14.0% on PassNet** than the identical agent without skills. New and material: the +134% comes *from adding verified operating context under a fixed setup* — i.e. the gain is the reusable skill, not a stronger agent or bigger budget. Directly ON-FOCUS: it is the "reusable, verified knowledge as the load-bearing lever" pole of the OpenEvolve algorithmicsuperintelligence / AI-for-Science loop, and it mirrors agpack's own skill idea (structured, auditable, reused) at the research-agent scale.

**ADJACENT (1 of 3) — Cliff: reward shaping from the *first* mistake improves RLVR reasoning**
Peixuan Han, Runhui Wang, Ketan Ramaneti, et al. — [arXiv:2609.02817](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.02817) · submitted 2026-09-02
RLVR's problem is coarse outcome rewards: once a rollout goes wrong, scoring the rest is "already conditioned on an invalid prefix," so most tokens carry little information. Cliff uses an off-the-shelf LLM teacher to find the *first mistake*, splits each rollout into a correct prefix (token-level + advantage) and incorrect suffix (− advantage), and shapes training around that split. Across 12 scenarios it consistently beats on-policy distillation by 15% and standard GRPO by 7%, *even with modest-capability teachers*. New and material: the insight is that "the first mistake is the only token that matters," collapsing supervision to a single cut-point rather than per-token outcome grading — a cheap, general improvement to any RLVR pipeline. ADJACENT (training-method, not a loop), but the "distill a good reasoning path, don't just grade the endpoint" move is structurally the inverse of the auditable-harness concern.

**ADJACENT (2 of 3) — Declarative Attention: LMs declare where to attend, inference engine obeys**
Namgyu Ho, Huzama Ahmad, Woosung Koh, et al. — [arXiv:2609.02737](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.02737) · submitted 2026-09-02
Long-context KV-cache scanning is O(N) per token, and the usual fix (proxy scores pre-selecting relevant tokens) still pays O(N). Takes the *intrinsic* tack — "wouldn't the model already know which parts of the context matter?" — and elicits the model to declare its attention inside its chain-of-thought, partitioning generation into `<global>` (full context), `<focus>` (one region), and `<local>` (recent output), with the engine parsing those declarations like tool calls and skipping the KV read. Zero-shot across 15 long-context tasks: **52.0% / 31.1% fewer attended tokens** on Gemma-4-31B and Qwen-3.6-27B for 1.27 / 2.75 pp accuracy drops that shrink with model scale. New and material: it opens a *new axis of sparse attention driven by the model itself* rather than a proxy scorer. ADJACENT (inference efficiency, no loop), but "the model knows where to look" is a mild cousin of the self-modifying-harness theme where structure decides attention.

**ADJACENT (3 of 3) — Nemotron competitive-coding specialization outscores the top human on an IOI set**
Aleksander Ficek, Sean Narenthiran, Mehrzad Samadi, et al. — [arXiv:2609.02849](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.02849) · submitted 2026-09-02
An end-to-end specialization pipeline (22,000 curated problems → synthetic reasoning traces → SFT + RL) produces Nemotron-3-Nano-CC (30B-A3B), whose GenCorrect feedback-driven test-time compute pushes it to 468 on IOI 2025 (gold threshold 438.3). The headline: a 550B-A55B Ultra-CC system evaluated *prospectively* under real human constraints (same time, no internet beyond allowed resources) scored **535.4 / 600, exceeding both the human gold threshold and the top human score of 498.27** — reportedly the first AI to beat the highest-scoring human on an IOI set. New and material: it is the "test-time compute + iterative self-correction" pole of reasoning at the competitive ceiling, and it's a *propective* holdout under human constraints rather than a re-run. ADJACENT (a coding-eval pipeline, not a new reasoning mechanism), but the "iteratively generate/evaluate/refine" loop that drives it is the same self-correcting structure worth watching at the far end of any reasoning loop.

## 3. Recursive self-improvement

**ON-FOCUS — SafeEvolve: harness–policy co-evolution with auditable, reversible safety artifacts**
Qinghua Mao, Wanying Qu, Dadi Guo, et al. — [arXiv:2609.02786](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.02786) · submitted 2026-09-02
Directly continues yesterday's "harness is load-bearing" thread (Harness-of-Harness 2609.01481) but from the *safety* side: LLM agent performance is jointly shaped by base model *and* harness, so both harmful final responses and multi-step execution trajectories are risk surfaces, and either harness-update or policy-optimization alone fails to bridge runtime control and intrinsic safety. SafeEvolve is an *experience-driven* loop: completed on-policy trajectories drive a continual **harness-policy co-evolution** — on the harness side, converting trajectory-level safety evidence into *bounded, component-level, auditable and reversible* updates (safety prompt + hierarchical skills); on the policy side, harness-use SFT bootstraps the policy to leverage the evolved harness, followed by harness-augmented RL with verifier-decomposed rewards. On agentic safety benchmarks it beats baselines with a stronger safety–utility tradeoff: 3× attack-success-rate reduction on AgentDojo with benign utility up 59.79% → 61.86% (Qwen3.5-4B). New and material: "auditable *and reversible* harness artifacts" is the load-bearing move — it turns the self-evolving harness that HoH *trusted* into one whose changes are bounded and undoable, closing the exact "how do I let the loop run before I can no longer verify/reverse it" hole. ON-FOCUS: the OpenWorker auditable-self-modification open question, now stated as a working safety architecture.

**ADJACENT (1 of 3) — Bilevel Coordinated Reflection: multi-agent LLM systems as a coordination game with an impossibility for transcript-only verification**
Yihang Chen, Yuxiang Chen, Yuxuan Huang, et al. — [arXiv:2609.02750](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.02750) · submitted 2026-09-02
Multi-agent LLM systems decompose with an orchestrator and improve via textual reflection, but lack a unified account tying coordination, memory improvement, and external verification together. Models the orchestrator–worker interaction as a *bilevel coordination game* (workers' local-update game is an approximate potential game, slack controlled by decomposition quality) and reflection as stochastic motion over semantic-memory states, then proves an **information-theoretic impossibility**: *no gate that observes only the generated transcript can improve uniformly* over text-indistinguishable environments — only an environment-grounded gate can. Motivated by this separation it deploys Stochastic Reflective Memory Ascent (accept a memory only when a grounded evaluation risk strictly decreases), converging geometrically/polynomially, and reports 72.2% resolved on 500 SWE-bench instances vs a 70.8% mini-SWE-agent reference. New and material: the *impossibility theorem for transcript-only verification* is the load-bearing result — it formally pins down exactly why "the loop only sees its own output" cannot guarantee improvement, and thus why a *grounded* external check is required. ADJACENT as an evaluation/theory artifact, but it is the theoretical backbone of the "see the failure, don't just trust the loop" concern across 09-02 and today.

### Cross-topic observation
Three threads collapse onto one: **the thing that makes a self-improving system progress is the *structure* around the model — and the structure must be auditable, reversible, or grounded, or the loop can't certify its own gains.** In quantum, that structure is literal: 2609.02641 builds a hardware+QEC+logical-compute co-design whose transversal gates and 15-to-1 magic-state distillation let a *single* platform run the whole fault-tolerant stack; 2609.02715 (parallel QAE) makes the *algorithm* structure geometrically efficient without losing the adaptive bound; 2609.02781 shows that a deployed QML classifier's own-shot output can't self-validate — it needs an auditable, fail-closed external check. In ML, the lever is reuse-of-context: Repo-To-Skill's +134% comes from *distilled verified skills* loaded under a fixed agent, and Cliff's gains come from *the one token that matters* rather than grading the endpoint. In RSI, SafeEvolve takes exactly the harness HoH trusted and makes it *auditable and reversible*, while Bilevel Coordinated Reflection *proves* that a transcript-only gate cannot certify progress — you need the grounded, auditable structure outside the loop. The recurring instruction for the reader: don't let the loop trust its own outputs; engineer the component — skill, gate, audit, harness — that makes the loop's improvement *checkable*.
