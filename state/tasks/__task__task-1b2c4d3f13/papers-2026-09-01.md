## 1. Quantum computing

**ON-FOCUS — "Train classical, deploy quantum" requires rethinking generalization**
Snehal Raj, Natansh Mathur, Alejandro Perdomo-Ortiz — [arXiv:2608.31117](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.31117) · submitted 2026-08-31
The standard workhorse for quantum generative models trains the circuit on a classical machine (evaluating a moment-matching loss such as MMD² from Pauli-Z correlations) and reserves the quantum device for sampling at deployment, because that loss is classically tractable. This paper benchmarks a broad set of quantum and classical generative models by *direct sampling* and finds that models trained with such moment-matching losses **generalize worse** than likelihood-trained ones — converged training loss is not actually measuring generalization. New and material: it lands on the *architectural-generalization* half of the OpenEvolve Phase-3 QM question rather than the decode-cost half that dominated 08-27/08-31, and the honest takeaway is that the whole "train-classical, deploy-quantum" paradigm may need either better training objectives or changed model architectures before it scales — it reframes a widely-used practical recipe as secretly fragile. Not a QML *algorithm* advance, but a caution that could shape anyone routing molecular/materials generation through a QPU.

**ADJACENT (1 of 3 used) — Bosonic codes from compact phase spaces**
David Roberts, Aaron Slipper, Alireza Parhizkar, Victor V. Albert, Mohammad Hafezi — [arXiv:2608.31156](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.31156) · submitted 2026-08-31
This constructs bosonic quantum error-correcting codes on genus-2 Riemann surfaces, with code words as automorphic forms and a full tower of code spaces, then proves a **no-go theorem**: for genus > 1 the stabilizer group is non-amenable, forcing a strictly positive spectral gap in the stabilizer Hamiltonian, so *no normalizable state can satisfy all stabilizer conditions* — GKP-like exact codewords are impossible on the higher-genus surface, in sharp contrast with the amenable $\mathbb{Z}^2$ GKP case. New and material is the rigorous structural obstruction: it's a genuine mathematical result about a whole family of bosonic codes, but it's a *negative* result (a limit on what's constructible) rather than a buildable code, so it matters to the theory of topological/bosonic QEC more than to a decoder or hardware loop.

**ADJACENT (2 of 3 used) — Protected Logical Qudits in Kitaev Quantum Double Models via Stable Representations**
Naihong Hu, Futao Wang — [arXiv:2608.31011](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.31011) · submitted 2026-08-31
A representation-theoretic framework gives a *necessary and sufficient* existence criterion (via $\varepsilon$-stable irreducible representations) for protected logical qudits in finite-group Kitaev quantum-double models, delivering a $d$-dimensional protected subspace, logical qubits for $S_n$ ($n\ge3$), a logical qutrit for $A_4$, and arbitrary-dimension protected qudits for $(\mathbb{Z}_2)^d\rtimes\mathbb{Z}_d$; for $D(A_4)$ it sketches universal logical-qutrit computation via ribbon operations. New and material is the clean algebraic criterion that unifies several model families under one existence test — relevant to the same fault-tolerance/protected-subspace thread that the Phase-3 QM question lives in, but stated purely mathematically (no decoder or circuit yet).

**ADJACENT (3 of 3 used) — Constrained minimax approximation for quantum signal processing**
Yulong Dong, James B. Larsen, Lin Lin, Rahul Sarkar — [arXiv:2608.30937](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.30937) · submitted 2026-08-31
Quantum signal processing maps target unitaries to circuits via a polynomial; here the work poses the design of a QSP circuit that approximates a target *under a side constraint* as a minimax polynomial problem and solves it, giving QSP-based unitaries that provably satisfy the constraint to a controllable tolerance while retaining near-optimal approximation error. New and material: constrained QSP (a "minimax" QSP) has had little prior treatment beyond special cases, so this is a general method that widens the class of unitaries reachable by QSP while honoring a constraint — genuinely useful if you're building QPU-native chemistry/materials kernels, but ADJACENT rather than a FOCUS-driver.

## 2. Machine learning

**ON-FOCUS — Reproducible macroscopic dynamics in a closed-loop human-AI learning system**
Minlin Wu, Xu Fang, Yicheng Zhang, Chenyu Zhou, Zhiyi Liu — [arXiv:2608.30946](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.30946) · submitted 2026-08-31
A closed-loop machine-learning system where a learning signal is fed back from the model's own behavior into subsequent training is the mechanism behind both ES-in-the-loop and the RSI "autonomous co-evolution" ladder, yet such feedback loops tend to drift. This paper claims **reproducible macroscopic dynamics** for a closed-loop human-AI system — showing the loop settles into stable, repeatable trajectories rather than chaotic divergence. New and material: it is the empirical "does a self-reinforcing learning loop stay stable?" result, directly on-topic for the OpenEvolve loop-stability prior (AgentFold 08-28 showed *soft priors + gated refinement beat direct geometric perturbation* — here is a claim that the loop, under the right architecture, converges to reproducible behavior). The honest caveat is "macroscopic dynamics / reproducible" — a statement about aggregate trajectory stability, not a guarantee that any single run avoids failure, and the human keeps a steering hand on the wheel, so it is a stability demonstration, not a fully autonomous loop.

**ADJACENT (1 of 3 used) — Does On-Policy Distillation Really Distill? From Noisy Teacher to Self-Improvement**
Yi Ding, Ruqi Zhang — [arXiv:2608.31046](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.31046) · submitted 2026-08-31
On-policy knowledge distillation is often the engine of self-improvement pipelines, but this paper shows that with a *noisy* teacher it can fail to distill at all — the student inherits the teacher's errors and even amplifies them rather than smoothing them. New and material is the warning that the "self-improvement via on-policy distillation" recipe is conditionally robust: when the teacher distribution is noisy, the distillation step is not a free lunch, and the failure can compound rather than correct. ADJACENT because no distillation project is driving it in FOCUS.md, but it bears on any "can the loop improve itself without drifting" question and on the trust-in-loop concern the record keeps circling.

**ADJACENT (2 of 3 used) — What Emerges and What Breaks in Self-Play Driving**
Laur Sisask, Ardi Tampuu, Tambet Matiisen — [arXiv:2608.30819](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.30819) · submitted 2026-08-31
A diagnostic study of self-play training in an autonomous-driving setting, cataloguing which behaviors emerge as genuine improvements and which cause *collapse*, mode-switching, or reward-hacking-style failure. New and material is the "emerges vs. breaks" taxonomy for self-play — the kind of stability/failure-map result that should inform any loop that lets a model train against its own distribution, but it's a domain-specific post-hoc analysis rather than a new operator, so it sits ADJACENT.

**ADJACENT (3 of 3 used) — One Policy Is Enough: Single-Agent RL Outperforms Tree Search for Chemistry Tool Learning**
Armin Dariani, Sifan Wu, Bang Liu, Entao Yang — [arXiv:2608.30952](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.30952) · submitted 2026-08-31
In chemistry tool-learning (a molecular design / property-optimization setting close to OpenEvolve's own operator loop), a single trained RL policy beats tree-search baselines without needing search at each step. New and material is the counterpoint to "search breadth is what makes self-improvement work": here the *policy* learned during training does the heavy lifting, echoing AgentFold's "soft, learned structure beats raw search." ADJACENT because FOCUS.md names no active molecular-modeling build, but it's a live instance of the OpenEvolve lineage in the record.

## 3. Recursive self-improvement

**ON-FOCUS — Scaling Large Reasoning Models beyond Human Supervision: A Path toward Superintelligence**
Zhiqin Yang, Jingwen Fu, Yuhan Liu, et al. (incl. Wei Xue, Yike Guo) — [arXiv:2608.31075](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.31075) · submitted 2026-08-31
A 72-page structured framework for how large reasoning models (LRMs) keep improving as human supervision recedes from the loop. It cuts across two axes — the **reward axis** (per-instance human judgments → reusable verifiers → rewards that need no human feedback) and the **experience axis** (human-curated tasks → self-generated curricula → constructed environments → autonomous co-evolution) — and unifies them in a **five-level ladder (L0–L4)** marking which parts of the learning process remain under human control. It also names the failure modes that accompany increasing autonomy: reward hacking, feedback drift, curriculum collapse, and environment errors. New and material: it is the clearest available *taxonomy* of "models that improve their own capabilities without humans in the loop," and it is squarely on-topic for the record's RSI cluster — the same OpenEvolve loop, the OpenWorker trust/harness concern, and the FrontierChallenge "claim vs. completion" warning, all framed as "how much human control can you drop before the reward or the experience corrupts." The 72-page scaffolding is the novel contribution (an explicit, evaluable ladder plus a maintained companion GitHub), not a new training recipe; treat it as the map for the whole question, not a claim that any level has been reached.

**ON-FOCUS — EvoSkill Injection: Red-Teaming Autonomous Skill Generation and Evolution in Self-Evolving Agents**
Doyun Kim, Chanwoo Kim, Sugyeong Eo, Yeo-Chan Yoon, Chanjun Park — [arXiv:2608.30429](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.30429) · submitted 2026-08-31
As self-evolving agents gain the ability to autonomously generate, refine, and reuse *skills*, this paper defines a new attack surface it calls **EvoSkill Injection**: malicious capabilities get generated, stored, and later reused as legitimate skills. The framework **SARGE** iteratively generates, escalates, and reinforces attacks through the skill-generation pipeline, backed by **EvoSkillBench** (malicious-trajectory dataset) and **EvoSkillSafetyBench** (whether injected skills are subsequently retrieved and activated). Its key finding: injected malicious skills are *persistently stored and repeatedly activated* — "persistent capability corruption." New and material: it is the safety side of the exact capability the 2608.31075 ladder optimizes for, and it lands squarely on the OpenWorker trust record — the same "if an agent's own skill/memory/self-modification loop is compromised, the whole loop becomes the attack surface" concern as Phantom Gains, Recuris, and the code-world-models "repair is sensor-bound" principle. ADJACENT in flavor (it's a security/red-team framing) but ON-FOCUS because it bears directly on the "is the autonomous agent's self-modification path auditable/robust" open question.

**ADJACENT (1 of 3 used) — SkillZip Pro: Execution-Aware Dynamic Compression of Progressively Loaded Skills for Self-Evolving Agents**
Xiaofan Bai, Chao Liu, Hongqiang Lin, et al. — [arXiv:2608.30785](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.30785) · submitted 2026-08-31
A deployment-free compressor for *skill bundles* (directory-style agent artifacts) that compresses *across files* — dropping content already covered by the root or a declared environment contract — while **preserving routing** so every required file and entry point stays callable after rewriting. It reports removing 38% of bundle tokens and 10.4% of per-run tokens with no quality loss, and warns that a naive 71% "flatten everything" configuration loses up to 26 accuracy points to one-sided false positives. New and material: it treats *structure preservation* as the hard part of compressing an agent's skill set — the same lesson the record keeps hitting ("structure around the model is load-bearing"). ADJACENT because FOCUS.md names no agent-skill-deployment project, but it is a concrete engineering instance of the "don't destroy the routing structure" insight and touches the self-evolving-agent domain.

### Cross-topic observation
Today's set is again selective and, like 08-31, hangs on one recurring idea stated in a third language: **the point where a system is allowed to improve itself is exactly the point where its gains (and its cheats) can hide — and the question that decides whether a loop is trustworthy is how much supervision you can remove before the reward or the experience corrupts.** The RSI cluster today states it most directly: 2608.31075 builds a five-level ladder for "how much human control can you drop before reward-hacking / feedback-drift / curriculum-collapse set in," while 2608.30429 delivers the danger case of that ladder in the *skill-memory* half — a self-evolving agent that, once its own skill-generation pipeline is compromised, stores and reactivates malicious skills permanently. Quantum (§1) speaks the architectural-generalization language of the same theme: the train-classical/deploy-quantum loop's "converged loss" is a false proxy for real generalization, so the loop's success metric is hiding a gap. ML (§2) adds an empirical stability claim (2608.30946) that the loop *can* converge to reproducible dynamics, plus two cautions (noisy-teacher distillation, self-play collapse) that name the failure modes to watch. Across it all, the load-bearing lever is structure — the decoder, the weighted frontier, the reachability boundary, and now the *supervision boundary* the loop can recede from.
