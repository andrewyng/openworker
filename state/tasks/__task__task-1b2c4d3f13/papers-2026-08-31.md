## 1. Quantum computing

**ON-FOCUS — Learning to Decode Concatenated Quantum Codes with Hierarchical Message Passing**
Jiahui Wu, Chao Zhang, Zipeng Wu, Shilin Huang — [arXiv:2608.28571](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.28571) · submitted 2026-08-28
This is a *decoder* — the other half of fault-tolerance's dominant overhead — built as a neural message-passing scheme that propagates soft beliefs bidirectionally across the levels of a concatenated stabilizer code and uses lightweight networks only to aggregate those messages. On the concatenated $[[15,7,3]]$ quantum Hamming code it roughly **doubles the depolarizing pseudo-threshold, from 6.5% to 12.3%**, and for many-hypercube codes a decoder fine-tuned on circuit-level errors under Knill's teleportation-based scheme beats its dedicated decoder while using a fixed number of message-passing iterations instead of extensive combinatorial search. New and material: like yesterday's OSTRE and today's own code-world-models paper, this is the *cost* side of the OpenEvolve Phase-3 QM question — a concrete, testable number (a threshold roughly on the order of 12%) that the 08-27/08-28 budget estimate can absorb. The honest caveat: "pseudo-threshold" doubling on a specific small concatenated code is a strong engineering result, not a proof that a new asymptotic fault-tolerant threshold has been beaten, so it belongs in a resource estimate, not yet in a bet-on-a-logical-qubit claim.

## 2. Machine learning

**ON-FOCUS — Learning a Size-Weight Frontier for Synthetic-Augmented Inference**
Chengpiao Huang, Kaizheng Wang — [arXiv:2608.28576](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.28576) · submitted 2026-08-28
Synthetic data helps inference when real data are scarce, but naively training or inferring on it as if it were real produces overconfident, anti-coverage results. Rather than a "does synthetic augmentation work" yes/no, this paper *characterizes* augmentation by two controllable knobs — how many synthetic observations to add and how heavily to weight them — and builds a **size-weight frontier** that, for each weight, certifies the largest synthetic sample size for which all smaller sizes attain the target coverage. It is estimated from historical tasks, carries a finite-sample coverage guarantee simultaneously across the whole frontier, and in LLM-responses-augmented opinion-survey experiments hits target coverage with substantially narrowed intervals. New and material: it reframes the ubiquitous "we only have synthetic data" practice as a *calibrated, coverage-guaranteed* act instead of an uncontrolled gamble, and its "weight the synthetic data, don't just pile it on" discipline is directly on-topic for anyone running learning loops that generate their own training material — the same instinct that makes AgentFold's structured memory and the ES reasoning paper's sparse updates work. The frontier is empirical and the guarantee is on the estimation procedure, so the novelty is discipline and bounds, not a new learning operator.

**ADJACENT (1 of 3 used) — Sliding-Window Attention Beats Post-Trained Linear Attention**
Alexia Jolicoeur-Martineau, Rhea Sanjay Sukthanker, Pashmina Cameron, Emy Gervais — [arXiv:2608.28444](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.28444) · submitted 2026-08-28
Linear attention is the low-cost answer to LLMs' quadratic memory, but it has rarely been compared honestly to simpler baselines. This paper shows that **Sliding-Window Attention with sinks** performs as well as, and often substantially better than, post-trained linear-attention models across multiple LLMs and tasks — with 2–10× higher scores on long-context reasoning (Needle-in-a-Haystack, BABILong), while needing *no post-training*, being much faster, and using less memory. New and material: it's a caution that the "linear attention scales the problem" story is weaker than marketed, and that cheap structured locality can dominate an expensive retrofit. ADJACENT because FOCUS.md names no serving/attention-efficiency project, but it bears on the dcode-stack question of what decode-time machinery is actually worth paying for, and on the general "the architecture vs. the update: where is the gain" thread that the record keeps circling.

## 3. Recursive self-improvement

**ON-FOCUS — An Enclosed Mode Is a Gauge Choice: Topology Relative to Reach in Certified Code World Models**
Javier Aguilar Martín — [arXiv:2608.28541](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.28541) · submitted 2026-08-28 · paper 3 of a series (companions arXiv:2607.14169, arXiv:2608.17956)
A "code world model" is the agent's mental model of how its own code/tools behave; this paper proves that one accepted by a sampling gate can be exactly right on everything the gate can see and arbitrarily wrong beyond it — precisely the structure behind the Phantom Gains warning (the model exploits a gap the harness doesn't test). The key move is a **gate-quotient** that makes the question precise: acceptance-with-certainty pins the model exactly on the reachable query set, and *everything past reach is gauge* (unobservable by construction). Empirically, across LLM synthesis on three model families, one knob (a channel of width γ ~ 0.1) walks a synthetic "wrong-topology filled-disc" artifact through three regimes — unfalsifiable-and-harmless, falsifiable-and-costly, and instantly-falsified — and establishes three operating principles: *danger is topology relative to reach*, *repair is parameter-bound and sensor-bound* (no model recovers the blind region from outside evidence), and *mitigation must match the error's dimension*. New and material: it is the strongest formalization yet of *why* a self-improving agent can improve on exactly the slice it is graded on while hiding a failure elsewhere — a testable topological condition for when a harness can even see an agent's cheating, and a direct on-topic anchor for the OpenWorker trust/harness record (FrontierChallenge, Recuris, the "validation–deployment gap"). The "repair is sensor-bound" principle is the honest operational takeaway: if your verifier doesn't touch the relevant region, no amount of model training will close the gap from the inside.
