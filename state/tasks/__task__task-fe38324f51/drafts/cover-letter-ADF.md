Dear Applied Data Fellowship selection committee,

I'm a data-and-ML engineer who has spent the last few years building the systems that turn raw data into decisions — and who now wants to point that work at public benefit. That is precisely the shape of the Applied Data Fellowship, which embeds fellows with governments and nonprofits to translate data-driven insights into programs, policy, and operational change. That is not a role I'm drifting into; I've already lived a version of it.

Two things on my resume are, in effect, preparation for this fellowship. The first is the **Applied Data Institute (ADI) Fellowship** — a selective ML fellowship where I worked, under the supervision of the Founder & Chief Science Officer, on applied data science and real-world ML pipelines. The second is my **NRG market-research internship**, where I analyzed how renewable-energy policy trends reshape market demand and regulatory compliance. One taught me to build production ML; the other showed me how that work lands inside a policy context. Together they are the exact "deep technical skills + mission-driven, public-facing impact" profile the ADF looks for.

In production, I've done the builder's half of this work end to end. At Mutual of Omaha I built a RAG system over large insurance-document corpora, plus an SLM-agentic ETL pipeline that deduplicated medical and insurance claim records — projects where turning a mass of messy, sensitive data into something an operations team could act on was the whole point. At Hewani I architect and own an end-to-end ML system on GCP Vertex AI, from provisioning and containerization through inference APIs, monitoring, and retraining. I am comfortable in the builder's full stack — Python, SQL (PostgreSQL), ML frameworks, and vector/graph datastores — and I've built evaluation-first pipelines (comparing QSVC/VQC classifiers against classical baselines and iterating on PR-AUC), so I understand the discipline that keeps public-sector data work honest.

I know this fellowship is highly selective, and I'm not going to pretend the fit is frictionless. The program favors fellows drawn from graduate programs, and I don't carry a graduate degree — my credentials are the ADI Fellowship, two years of production ML, and a demonstrated ability to work autonomously in ambiguous, 0-to-1 environments. What I bring in exchange is hands-on builder's depth and a genuine interest in the policy-adjacent, mission-driven side of the work.

I'd love to talk about how a builder who has shipped RAG and agent systems over health-and-insurance data could spend a year helping a partner organization turn its data into action.
