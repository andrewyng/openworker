Dear MATS Residency Selection Committee,

I am applying to the Winter 2027 MATS Residency because I want to do real research on making
advanced AI safe, and I have spent the last few years building AI systems end-to-end — the
hands-on ability to run experiments, implement models, and debug my own code — and the honest
curiosity about where the technology goes that I now want to point toward safety.

My ML work is practical and complete, from idea to deployment. At Hewani I architected and
deployed an end-to-end recommendation system on Google Cloud Vertex AI, owning the full
lifecycle from infrastructure provisioning through containerization to monitoring and
retraining. At Mutual of Omaha I built a retrieval-augmented-generation system over insurance
document corpora and an agentic small-language-model ETL pipeline that deduplicates medical
and insurance claims. These are not API wrappers — they are systems where I had to design
the model, the data, and the evaluation, and then make them reliable in production.

More relevantly for MATS, I have been building at the frontier edge of a field that is
itself raising alignment-adjacent questions. My hybrid quantum-classical ML project compares
QSVC and VQC models against classical baselines on a biomedical knowledge graph, choosing
models on measured PR-AUC rather than assumption — the discipline of an evaluator, not just a
builder. I have run quantum circuits against real IBM hardware via Qiskit and PennyLane, and I
built a post-quantum random-number generator validated against the NIST test suites. That
combination — implementing models, running them, and rigorously checking whether they actually
do what I claimed — is exactly the "strong ML background; run experiments, implement papers,
debug your own code" bar you describe.

I know I am not a traditional candidate: there is no PhD, no formal "researcher" title, no
publication list behind me. That is deliberate. I am a self-taught engineer — an ADI
Applied-Data Fellow and, before that, a full-stack Java academy graduate — who has learned to
ship AI for real, and I want to apply that to the questions I find most urgent. I have
already spent time thinking about where AI is going, through hackathons, an AI-Society
membership, and projects where I deliberately chose the path with the harder-to-justify
metrics. I take initiative, I work autonomously, and I can show progress at the end of a week.

The MATS Residency is where I would rather learn than watch. Give me the problem and I will
get to work at industry speed — and I will bring the habits of someone who has had to make AI
systems trustworthy in production, not just in a notebook.

Thank you for your consideration.

## Why this letter works for the profile
- **No-degree honesty, not avoidance.** The applicant has no degree. The letter states it
  directly and reframes it as the program's stated preference: "Any career stage: ...
  self-taught researchers, or people transitioning into AI alignment." It turns the single
  weakness into the fit.
- **Front-loads the "run experiments, implement, debug" bar** — the program's explicit core
  requirement — with Hewani + Mutual of Omaha, which are complete, non-trivial systems.
- **The QML project is the bridge to safety.** It is evidence of evaluation discipline,
  honest model choice on metrics, and frontier-adjacent work — enough to make the "no PhD"
  bar credible without inventing alignment experience.
- **Pre-empts the "no publications/alignment experience" gap** by leaning on initiative
  (hackathons, AI Society, self-directed projects), which is exactly the alternative evidence
  the posting invites.
