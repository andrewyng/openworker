Dear GovAI Talent Development Team,

I am writing to apply for the GovAI Seasonal Fellowship on the **Applied track**. I build and deploy production ML systems at scale on Google Cloud, and I want to apply that builder's discipline to the concrete problem of making frontier AI systems governable, evaluable, and safe to operate. Your mission — helping humanity navigate the transition to a world with advanced AI — is the direction I have been building toward, and the Applied-track fellowship is the first programme I have seen that treats technical deployment capability as a governance asset rather than a downstream afterthought.

A few specifics about what I bring:

**I ship evaluated ML systems, not prototypes.** As an ML Solutions Architect at Hewani I architected and deployed an end-to-end recommendation system on Google Cloud Vertex AI — Feature Store, Matching Engine, and AI Platform — owning the full lifecycle from infrastructure provisioning through monitoring and retraining. I then designed and exposed RESTful inference APIs consumed across multiple application surfaces. That is the Applied-track skill set: taking a model from a working artifact to something an organisation can actually depend on at scale.

**I understand evaluation as a discipline on frontier models.** My personal research in hybrid quantum-classical ML built a pipeline on the Hetionet biomedical knowledge graph, benchmarking quantum (QSVC/VQC) and classical (logistic regression, SVM, random forest) classifiers and selecting on PR-AUC (~0.73), validated against real IBM Quantum hardware (Brisbane, Torino) via Qiskit and PennyLane. The habit that project trained — comparing methods honestly, reporting what the eval actually measures, and knowing when a model is good enough to trust — is exactly the instinct governance work needs: you cannot govern what you cannot measure.

**I have already deployed agentic systems.** At Mutual of Omaha I built an ETL pipeline that used Small Language Models as autonomous agents to deduplicate medical and insurance claim records, and a RAG system for semantic search over large insurance-document corpora. So I have stood at the exact intersection the Applied track sits in — deploying autonomous and retrieval-based systems that real people rely on — and I feel the gaps, the failure modes, the points where an evaluation or a guardrail would have changed the outcome.

What I am looking for is a structured, high-signal environment where that builder's instincts are turned toward the problems that matter: evaluations, interpretability, robustness, safeguards. I have spent two years learning how to build ML systems that work; the fellowship is the next step — learning how to judge whether they *should*, and how to make them governable in practice.

I am a US-based applicant, and I understand the 3-month UK programme is in-person; I would want to be upfront about the logistics rather than leave them for the interview. I am prepared to make the arrangement work, and I am asking in good faith for the chance to be considered on the Applied track rather than on the basis of any formal credential — because, as I read your call, you judge fellows on what they can actually do, not on a title.

Thank you for your time. I have attached my resume and would welcome the chance to discuss how I could contribute to the cohort.
