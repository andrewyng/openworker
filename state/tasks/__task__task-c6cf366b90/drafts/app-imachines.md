## Why it *can* work for you

- IMI explicitly **welcomes self-taught engineers transitioning into research** and
  says "we do not discriminate on the basis of pedigree." No PhD required.
- Your two open, shipped, benchmarked projects (Hetionet QML with a live demo;
  post-quantum RNG validated against NIST suites) are genuine *self-directed*
  research artifacts with public code — the closest proxy to the "self-directed
  research" bar they ask for.
- Remote, worldwide. Stipend + conference/travel + a publication-encouraged culture;
  past fellows have published at NeurIPS/ICML/ICLR and joined IMI full-time.
- 10 seats; two cohort deadlines (April 1 past, **Sept 1 open**), 3-week decision.

## What a credible application must contain (IMI's required format)
IMI's application = a brief bio/CV **plus**: (1) the topic you want to work on,
(2) a few lines on relevant prior work, (3) github/scholar/x links, (4) desired
start date, duration, obligations, and (5) **a brief analysis of one of their listed
projects** (or your own idea with time + compute estimate).

Be honest about which of these are strong and which are thin:

- **(2) relevant prior work** = your two shipped projects. Fine, but IMI's listed
  topics are frontier-ML internals (faster learners / approximate analytic SGD,
  learn-to-database, neural-to-program compilation, prefix advantage distillation)
  — **not** quantum ML or applied RAG. Your prior work is strong *engineering*
  research, not frontier-ML-internal research. Frame it as "I ship self-directed,
  measured research," which is what they say they're actually testing.
- **(5) project analysis** is the make-or-break. You need to pick *one* of IMI's
  projects and give a plausible, technically-specified analysis (what works, what
  likely fails, your estimate / gaps). This is a genuinely hard bar and where a
  weak draft will sink the application. Budget real time for it, or skip the role.
- **Publications:** you have none. Do not fabricate. If you want to improve the
  shot, the single highest-leverage move (optional, not required) is turning the
  Hetionet QML result into a short write-up / preprint or a detailed open README
  with a clear methodology, baseline table, and ablation — that is a much stronger
  "self-directed research" signal than a blog post. Do this *before* applying, not
  as part of the draft.
