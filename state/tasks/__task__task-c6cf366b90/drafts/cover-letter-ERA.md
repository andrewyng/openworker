# Cover Letter — Cambridge ERA:AI Fellowship 2027

Applicant: Jonathan Beale · Position: ERA:AI Winter 2027 Fellowship · Deadline: **13 September 2026, 11:59pm AoE**

Dear ERA:AI Fellowship Committee,

The question I keep circling is how makeable "dangerous capability" actually is — whether I can measure, and if necessary constrain, an autonomous system whose objective only partially aligns with the operator's. My answer has been to build, test, and break things across the frontier edge of what's currently accessible, and to insist that I pick the model on what it *does*, not on what it's named. That is the mindset your fellowship is built around, and it's why I'm applying.

I do not have the conventional credentials for a research fellowship — no degree, no publications. Your programme is explicitly talent-first, and that framing is honest about how I got here. My technical competence was earned building full systems end to end, not completing a syllabus.

At Hewani I architected and deployed an end-to-end ML recommendation system on GCP Vertex AI — from infrastructure provisioning through containerization, live inference APIs, monitoring, and retraining — and at Mutual of Omaha I built a retrieval-augmented system over insurance-document corpora plus an agentic SLM pipeline that deduplicates claims. On the "agentic oversight" front, I've spent time watching autonomous agents act over messy data and deciding when the system was behaving in ways its operators didn't intend. That is the practical substrate of the research you're describing.

My quantum-classical work is where I moved from building toward *evaluating*. In the Hybrid QML biomedical link-prediction project, I did not just deploy a QSVC and a VQC model — I compared them against logistic regression, SVM, and random forest on the same Hetionet knowledge graph, and selected on PR-AUC (~0.73), where QSVC led. I pushed quantum embeddings through RotatE representations and LDA, and ran the circuits against real IBM Quantum hardware (Brisbane, Torino) via Qiskit. Separately, for a post-quantum RNG I benchmarked against classical PRNGs and validated statistical randomness with the NIST suites. The discipline I learned — treat the model as a hypothesis, let the eval decide, report what actually won — is exactly the empirical rigor your fellows are expected to bring to safety questions about robustness, evaluation, and dangerous capability.

I want to point that skill set at the frontier rather than stop at building products on top of it. Your fellowship's research across evaluations, interpretability, robustness, and control is the place I'd rather apply my judgment. And because I'm a builder, I can be an independent worker from day one: I've shipped everything I've built, debugging my own code and refusing to ship what the eval didn't justify.

I understand the logistics require a 10-week presence in Cambridge (the fellowship starts 18 January 2027), and I'm prepared to commit to that. I would welcome the chance to work with a mentor on a concrete research project and to help make the risks of capable AI systems something we can actually measure.

Thank you for considering my application.
