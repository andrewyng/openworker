# Cover Letter — CBAI Fall Research Fellowship in AI Safety ('26)

> Tailored for Jonathan Beale. Written 2026-09-04 to the official window: **Oct 13 – Dec 18 2026, deadline September 6, 11:59 PM Eastern**. Host: Cambridge Boston Alignment Initiative (Harvard Square, Cambridge, MA).

---

Dear CBAI Selection Committee,

I am writing to apply for the Fall Research Fellowship in AI Safety. I build production ML
systems for a living and, outside of them, I have spent the last two years running
honest evaluations against frontier technology — benchmarks I let the hardware decide
rather than the abstract, and interpretation work grounded in real circuits. CBAI's
mission — supporting researchers who advance the safety and responsible governance of
advanced AI through technical and governance work in the heart of the AI-safety
community — is the direction I have already been moving in, and I want to turn that
trajectory into full-time work.

What I bring is a builder's instinct, sharpened by an evaluator's discipline.

As an ML Solutions Architect I architect and deploy end-to-end ML systems on Google Cloud
Vertex AI, owning the full lifecycle from provisioning to monitoring and retraining — and,
most recently, I have been building the kind of agentic, rule-bound system that sits
squarely at the intersection of capability and safety: LLMs paired with deterministic,
auditable "guardrail" logic so that automated decisions stay interpretable, verifiable,
and within policy. That is the same shape as CBAI's interest in verifiability, evaluation
frameworks, and keeping a human able to trace *why* a system did what it did.

My personal research — a hybrid quantum-classical ML pipeline on the Hetionet biomedical
knowledge graph — is the concrete evidence of my evaluation discipline. I did not choose
QSVC and VQC because they were novel; I chose them because I wanted to know, on real IBM
Quantum hardware via Qiskit and PennyLane, whether a parametrised quantum model could hold
its own against classical regressions. I benchmarked, interpreted, and reported honestly —
PR-AUC ~0.73 for QSVC, and a clear account of what the quantum and classical baselines did
and did not get right. Separately I built a post-quantum random-number generator validated
against NIST randomness suites, where a wrong answer is a security flaw, not a soft score.
That is the mindset I would bring to CBAI's interpretability, formal verification, or
risk-management tracks: measure first, claim only what the evidence supports, and make the
result auditable.

I am especially drawn to CBAI because the program is built on close mentorship with the
people actually doing the work — researchers from Harvard, MIT, Northeastern, METR,
Goodfire, and elsewhere — and on a cohort that treats both technical and governance
questions as serious work. I want to learn the method from people who publish it, and to
contribute the practical engineering instincts (rigorous evaluation, careful instrumentation,
honest reporting) that make that method trustworthy.

I understand the fellowship runs 10 weeks in Cambridge, Massachusetts, and that CBAI does
not sponsor visas. I am based in the United States and have the right to work here, so the
logistics of joining you in person for the full period are straightforward for me. I am
available for the entire cohort (October 13 – December 18) and eager to bring my full
attention to it.

Thank you for considering my application. I would welcome the chance to contribute to the
program's research, workshops, and community.

Sincerely,
Jonathan Beale

  but a strong letter that leads with *your* evaluation discipline is the right anchor.
- **Pick one track to name.** The page lists technical (interpretability, formal verification,
  multi-agent safety), governance, and biosecurity. Lead with whichever is your genuine
  pull; the letter above hedges slightly, so commit on the form.

  this is *domestic*. You do not need visa sponsorship; CBAI's own page says they "are unable
  to sponsor visas for this program," and it runs Oct–Dec 2026, before the J-1 sponsorship they
  mention for the *Spring* cycle. So there is no visa barrier for you.
- **Deadline: Sept 6, 11:59 PM Eastern.** Same day as MATS's general window and its Neel Nanda
  stream (which closes today, Sept 4). Prioritise this and MATS this week.
